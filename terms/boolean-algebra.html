Boolean algebra

  • Boolean algebra = algebra of two values \(\{0,1\}\) with operations AND (·), OR (+), NOT (′ or bar)

From Digital Logic › Boolean Algebra — Definitions and Axioms

Boolean algebra was designed for logic, with two values and three operations. Two features make it different from ordinary algebra. First, \(x+x=x\) and \(x\cdot x=x\), since repeating a statement adds nothing. Second, OR distributes over AND (\(x+yz=(x+y)(x+z)\)), which has no counterpart in ordinary algebra.

From Digital Logic › Basic Theorems and Properties

Prove using Boolean algebra: (a) \(A+AB=A\) (b) \(A+A'B=A+B\) (c) the consensus theorem \(AB+A'C+BC=AB+A'C\).
