Term · DLD Lab, Digital Logic

De morgan

  • Universal gates: NAND and NOR can build any gate. With NAND: NOT \(=\overline{A\cdot A}\); AND \(=\overline{\overline{AB}}\) (NAND followed by NAND-as-NOT); OR \(=\overline{\overline{A}\cdot\overline{B}}\) (De Morgan). With NOR: NOT \(=\overline{A+A}\); OR \(=\overline{\overline{A+B}}\); AND \(=\overline{\overline{A}+\overline{B}}\)

From DLD Lab › Experiment 1 — Logic gates using ICs; NAND and NOR as universal gates

  • Complement of a function: apply De Morgan, or take dual and complement each literal
    • \(F=x'yz'+x'y'z\) → \(F'=(x+y'+z)(x+y+z')\)

From Digital Logic › Boolean Functions

  • De Morgan for \(n\) variables: \((ABC\ldots)'=A'+B'+C'+\ldots\) and \((A+B+C+\ldots)'=A'B'C'\ldots\)

From Digital Logic › Basic Theorems and Properties

  • To get POS: group the 0s → get \(F'\) in SOP → complement with De Morgan

From Digital Logic › Product-of-Sums Simplification

Many commercial decoders have active-low outputs, which means the selected output is 0 and the others are 1. When you use such a decoder to build a function, you combine the outputs with a NAND gate instead of an OR gate, because NAND of active-low terms equals OR of the active-high ones (De Morgan).
