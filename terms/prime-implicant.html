Prime implicant

  • Prime implicants: \(A'C'D\) (1,5), \(A'BD\) (5,7), \(A'BC\) (6,7), \(B'D'\) (0,2,8,10), \(B'C'\) (0,1,8,9), \(CD'\) (2,6,10,14)

From Digital Logic › Tabulation (Quine–McCluskey) Method

  • Prime implicant (PI): an implicant that cannot be made bigger (a maximal group). Not contained in a larger group

From Digital Logic › The Map Method — Karnaugh Map (K-map)

Prime implicants: \(000-=A'B'C'\) (0,1); \(-0-0=B'D'\) (0,2,8,10); \(1-1-=AC\) (10,11,14,15).

From Digital Logic Design Unit II questions › question

\(2^4=16\) cells. A prime implicant is a product term (a group of 1s) that cannot be combined into a larger group. An essential prime implicant covers a minterm that no other prime implicant covers.
