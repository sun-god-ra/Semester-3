Sign test

To pick the test, ask what the data look like. One sample or a paired sample compared with a middle value: sign test. Two independent samples: Mann–Whitney \(U\). Three or more independent samples: Kruskal–Wallis \(H\) (the \(U\) test generalised). Two numeric measurements per item and you want association: Spearman. A single sequence and you want to know if the order is random: runs test.

From Prob & Stats › Worked Examples from the Lecture Slides (Dr M. V. Rao)

  • Assumes differences come from a symmetric distribution (sign test doesn't)

From Prob & Stats › Wilcoxon Signed-Rank Test (extension of sign test)

The sign test is the simplest test there is. For one sample, we ask whether the sample median equals a claimed value. If it does, each observation is equally likely to fall above or below it, so the count of observations above is binomial with \(p=\tfrac12\). For paired data we do the same on the differences. Values equal to the claimed median give no sign and are removed, which lowers \(n\). Forgetting to remove ties is a common error.
