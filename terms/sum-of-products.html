Sum of products

A minterm is a product term that is 1 for exactly one combination of the inputs. To get a function from its truth table we OR the minterms of all the rows where the function is 1. This gives the canonical sum of products. A maxterm is a sum that is 0 for exactly one input combination, and the function is the AND of the maxterms for the rows where it is 0. Both forms describe the same function, so their index lists are complementary, which is a useful check on an answer.

Grouping the 1s gives a sum of products. To get a product of sums, group the 0s instead. That simplifies the complement \(F'\), and by De Morgan's law \(F=(F')'\) turns the sum of products of \(F'\) into a product of sums. When you read a sum term directly from a group of 0s, the sign is reversed compared with SOP: a variable that is 0 throughout the group is written uncomplemented, since the sum must be 0 there. Choose POS or SOP according to whichever has fewer groups, or whichever matches the gates you have.
