Term · DLD Lab, Digital Logic

Johnson counter

  • Each output is a square wave at \(f/8\) (period = 8 clocks), and each is a copy of the previous one delayed by one clock (a 4-FF Johnson counter = divide-by-8, 8 phases)

From Digital Logic › Johnson (Twisted-Ring) Counter

  • Self-starting: the ring counter and the Johnson counter must be initialised (preset or clear), otherwise they may start in an unused state and stay in an invalid loop (the 4-bit Johnson counter has 16 − 8 = 8 unused states that form separate loops), or they need extra gating to self-correct

From DLD Lab › Experiment 10 — Ring counter and Johnson counter

  • Generated by: (1) ring counter; (2) \(n\)-bit counter + \(n\)-to-\(2^n\) decoder (fewer FFs, but decoder glitches); (3) Johnson counter + AND gates

From Digital Logic › Timing Sequences

  • Uses: serial data communication, delay, ring/Johnson counters, multiplication and division by 2 (shift left or right), sequence generators

From DLD Lab › Experiment 11 — Shift registers: SISO, SIPO, PISO and PIPO

The Johnson counter feeds back the complement of the last output. The register fills up with 1s from one end, and then fills with 0s, so it goes through \(2n\) states with \(n\) flip-flops. Any one of the \(2n\) states can be recognised from two adjacent flip-flop outputs, so each decoded signal needs only a 2-input AND gate. The remaining \(2^n-2n\) states form separate loops, so a self-correcting circuit or a reset is required.
