Spearman

The same data gave \(t=4.03\) in the paired \(t\) test and \(r_s=0.988\) in the Spearman test. All three methods reject. The sign test uses only the direction of each difference, so it is the weakest of the three but needs no normality assumption.

From Prob & Stats › Worked Examples from the Lecture Slides (Dr M. V. Rao)

Pearson's coefficient measures straight-line association and is thrown off by outliers. Spearman's coefficient replaces the values with their ranks, so it measures whether one variable tends to rise when the other rises, whatever the shape of the curve. If the ranks agree exactly, every \(d_i\) is zero and \(r_s=1\). The more the ranks disagree, the larger \(\sum d_i^2\) and the smaller \(r_s\). The formula is just Pearson's \(r\) computed on the ranks, simplified for the case without ties.
