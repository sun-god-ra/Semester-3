Home › Terms A–Z › Propagation delay

Term · DLD Lab, Digital Logic

Propagation delay

  • Positive / negative logic, fan-in, fan-out, propagation delay, noise margin — gate characteristics

From Digital Logic › Other Logic Operations and Logic Gates

  • Advantages: simple, few gates. Disadvantages: propagation delays add up (\(n\,t_{pd}\)) → slower, glitches in decoding intermediate states → max clock frequency \(f_{max}=\dfrac{1}{n\,t_{pd}}\)

From Digital Logic › Asynchronous (Ripple) Counters

  • Disadvantage of ripple counters: the propagation delays add, so at high frequency the count is wrong momentarily (decoding glitches); the maximum clock frequency \(f_{max}=1/(n\cdot t_{pd})\). Advantage: simple hardware

From DLD Lab › Experiment 9 — Asynchronous (ripple) counters: up, down and MOD-N

  • JK: 00 hold, 01 reset, 10 set, 11 toggle — no forbidden state. Race-around: when J=K=1 and the clock pulse is longer than the propagation delay, Q toggles repeatedly in one pulse. Cures: master-slave construction (two latches, the master is active while CLK=1 and the slave while CLK=0, so the output changes only on the falling edge), or edge triggering

From DLD Lab › Experiment 7 — Latches and flip-flops: SR latch, SR, JK, D, T and master-slave JK

  • Timing parameters: setup time (input stable before the edge), hold time (after the edge), propagation delay \(t_{pd}\)
