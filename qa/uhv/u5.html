Part B · Unit V

Implications on Professional Ethics

Universal Human Values II · 4 questions · Notes for this unit

Q1Apr 20268MCO5 K2

Mention the four steps for facilitating the understanding of harmony at various levels. Explain any two in detail.

Answer

(⚠ The paper's "four steps" are not stated in my notes. The usual reading is the four levels at which harmony is to be understood and lived. Check the wording against your faculty notes before the exam.) The four levels: (1) the human being (myself: 'I' and the body), (2) the family, (3) the society, (4) nature and existence. Each level is understood and then lived through the sequence: right understanding → right feeling → right thought → right behaviour and work.

  1. Harmony in the human being: 'I' is in harmony with the body (Sanyam and health); the 'I' has no contradiction between desire, thought and expectation. It gives continuous happiness.
  2. Harmony in the family: mutual trust and respect among members, justice, care, affection and the nine values; physical facility is shared with sufficiency (prosperity). This is the basic unit of the larger order.
  3. Society: extends the same into undivided society, with the five dimensions.
  4. Nature and existence: mutual fulfilment with the four orders through recyclability and self-regulation; co-existence. Harmony at each level depends on the others.
Q2Apr 20268MCO5 K2

Explain various issues in professional ethics in the current scenario.

Answer

Professional ethics = right conduct in professional work, based on human values. Present issues:

  1. Quality and safety: bridge or building collapses, software bugs, unsafe medical devices; profit before safety.
  2. Honesty and integrity: falsifying data, test reports, plagiarism, cheating.
  3. Conflict of interest, bribery and corruption.
  4. Confidentiality and misuse of data or privacy.
  5. Environmental harm: pollution, e-waste, unsustainable production.
  6. Exploitation of workers, unfair labour and discrimination.
  7. Intellectual property and unauthorised use.
  8. Whistle-blowing dilemma and pressure from employers.
  9. Obsolescence by design and commercialisation of education and health. Root causes: the wrong notion that professional excellence is wealth generation, competition, no value education. Remedy: ethical conduct with definite basis (values, policy, character) and professional competence directed towards the universal human order.
Q3Apr 20268MCO5 K4

(OR) Propose some case studies that are helpful in developing people-friendly and eco-friendly production systems.

Answer

(Illustrative; use the case studies your faculty gave if different.)

  • Biogas plant from cattle dung and kitchen waste: gives fuel and manure, waste becomes input, and reduces smoke and deforestation.
  • Rooftop solar / small hydro: renewable energy, decentralised, low pollution.
  • Rainwater harvesting and watershed management: conserves water, supports self-regulation.
  • Amul-type cooperatives: production owned by the producers, fair price, trust-based management, local jobs.
  • Appropriate technology (Schumacher, "small is beautiful") and Gandhian khadi / village industries: labour-friendly, local resources, durable.
  • Organic / natural farming: avoids chemical pollution, recycles crop residues.
  • Reuse and recycling units (paper, plastic, e-waste recycling): closes the loop. Features checked for each: people-friendly (meets needs, safe, creates meaningful work, fair, durable) and eco-friendly (renewable, recyclable, low pollution, within nature's regulation).
Q4Apr 20268MCO5 K2

(OR) Summarize the basis for humanistic education and humanistic constitution.

Answer
  • Basis: the natural acceptance of human values (universal, same in all) and the right understanding of the human being, family, society, nature and existence.
  • Humanistic education gives values and skills together, to make a responsible human being (not only a skilled worker). It develops right understanding first, then skills, so that competence serves the universal human order.
  • Humanistic constitution is the framework of society based on these values. It ensures the five dimensions (education, health, justice, production, exchange) for all and fulfils the four goals (resolution, prosperity, fearlessness, co-existence).
  • Chain: natural acceptance of values → humanistic education → humanistic constitution → universal human order (world family).
