Part B · Unit III

Harmony in the Family and Society

Universal Human Values II · 4 questions · Notes for this unit

Q1Apr 20268MCO3 K2

Define trust according to UHV. Explain with any two suitable examples.

Answer

Trust (vishwas) = assuming that the other is intrinsically capable of being right: that his intention is as good as mine (he wants to be happy and make others happy, as natural acceptance is the same in all) and that he has the competence potential to improve. It is the first value in relationship. It has two parts: intention (what the other wants to be, good in all) and competence (ability to do, which can be improved). We trust the intention and help with the competence.

Example 1, teacher–student: a student gets poor marks. Mistrust says "he doesn't want to study". Trust says "he wants to do well but is lacking in competence, let me help", and the student improves. Example 2, parent–child: a child breaks a glass; the parent who judges the intention ("careless on purpose") creates fear; the parent who trusts the intention ("she was trying to help") teaches the competence (how to carry). Example 3, shopkeeper: a customer wants a fair price; both are assumed to want mutual benefit. Result: both feel secure and develop; the absence of trust leads to fear, insecurity and conflict.

Q2Apr 20268MCO3 K2

With reference to the UHV course content, define Respect and Love and distinguish them from the existing notions in society.

Answer

Respect (samman) = right evaluation: accepting and valuing the other as he is (his potential and competence), neither over- nor under-evaluating. It is the same for everyone because it is on the 'I'. Love (prem) = the feeling of being related to all human beings; trust and respect extended to everyone, "the world is one family" (vasudhaiva kutumbakam). It is the complete value that includes all the others.

UHV meaning Present notion in society
Respect right evaluation of the person as he is; same for all differentiation on the basis of body (age, gender), physical facility (wealth, post) or belief (caste, religion); giving more importance to some; flattery or formal courtesy
Love feeling of relatedness to all, with trust and respect attachment to a few, based on body or give and take (family, friend); infatuation or physical attraction; "love" as a feeling of possession
Q3Apr 20268MCO3 K2

(OR) List the dimensions that comprise a human order. Explain how each contributes to the fulfilment of the human goal.

Answer

Five dimensions of the universal human order, and the goal each fulfils:

  1. Education–Right living (shiksha-sanskar): gives right understanding to every human being; leads to resolution (continuous happiness, ability to solve problems).
  2. Health–Self-regulation (swasthya-sanyam): Sanyam and health of the body, and of society; supports prosperity.
  3. Justice–Preservation (nyay-suraksha): justice in relationships and safeguarding of the human beings and nature; gives fearlessness (trust).
  4. Production–Work (utpadan-karya): producing the needed physical facility with human labour and in harmony with nature; leads to prosperity.
  5. Exchange–Storage (vinimay-kosh): fair exchange and storage of resources, for mutual fulfilment; supports co-existence. Together they realise the four goals: Resolution, Prosperity, Fearlessness (Trust) and Co-existence, and the aspirations of happiness and prosperity for all.
Q4Apr 20268MCO3 K2

(OR) Explain the meaning of "universal human order". Explain its relation to the family order.

Answer

Universal human order (sarvabhaum vyavastha) = an order in which all human beings live in harmony as an undivided society (akhand samaj): related by trust and respect, with no divisions of caste, creed, race, nation or class; it ensures the five dimensions and fulfils the four goals for everyone. In short, a world family.

Relation to family order: the family is the basic unit where relationships of trust and respect are first lived (mutual happiness, prosperity, justice, fearlessness). When the same values are extended step by step, the order grows: family → family cluster (extended family) → village → town → region → nation → world family.

So the universal human order is the family order extended to all humanity; if relationships fail in the family, a harmonious society is not possible.
