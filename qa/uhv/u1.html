Part B · Unit I

Introduction, Need, and Basic Guidelines

Universal Human Values II · 4 questions · Notes for this unit

Q1Apr 20268MCO1 K2

Explain the process of self-exploration with a neat diagram. Describe the content of self-exploration.

Answer

Self-exploration is the process of finding out what is right for me by investigating my own inner reality (desires, thoughts, expectations) and the outside reality (relationships, nature). It is a process of verification: nothing is accepted or rejected blindly; every proposal is checked on my own right.

Process (how): a proposal is checked with natural acceptance ("do I accept it from within?") and then with experiential validation (does it give continuous happiness and harmony when I live it, in thought, behaviour and work?). The two together form mutual verification; if a gap is found between "what I want to be" and "what I am", I correct my living.

Content (what to explore): (1) What do I want to be? my goal and aspirations; (2) What is my programme (how am I living) to achieve it?; (3) Am I able to fulfil it with what I am doing now? In short: What is the human being, what does he want, what should he do to get it? The aim: remove the gap between "to be done" and "what I am doing".

Q2Apr 20268MCO1 K2

Mention the basic human aspirations and indicate their correct priority. Explain the requirements to fulfil them.

Answer

Basic aspirations: continuous happiness (a state of no contradiction, harmony in me) and continuous prosperity (the feeling of having more physical facility than required).

Requirements and their correct priority:

  1. Right understanding (first): clarity of what is true about myself, others, nature, existence. Gives continuous happiness; qualitative; no limit.
  2. Relationship (second): living with trust and respect with all humans; qualitative, no limit.
  3. Physical facility (third): food, clothing, shelter, medicine, tools. Needed for the body, with a limit (quantity), gives temporary happiness.

Today we run after physical facility alone and neglect the first two, so we have conflict and crisis. Happiness comes from the first two (and from the third only in the right proportion); prosperity from correct appraisal of the need and production above it.

Q3Apr 20268MCO1 K3

(OR) Physical facilities are necessary and complete for animals, while they are necessary but not complete for humans. Justify.

Answer
  • Animal: its programme is for the body only (food, shelter, safety, continuation). Its 'I' (limited) lives as per breed, with fixed behaviour; no conflict or undefined desires. So when its body needs are met, it is fulfilled: facility is necessary and complete.
  • Human being: is 'I' + body. The body needs physical facilities (necessary), but the 'I' wants happiness, which needs right understanding and relationship (trust, respect, etc.). Physical facility cannot supply these. We see it in people who have everything, yet are stressed, lonely and insecure. So facility is necessary but not complete.
  • Also: the body's need is limited, but the 'I's wants for facility are unlimited when misguided, so the desire for facility alone can never be fulfilled.
Animal Human
lives for the body lives as 'I' using a body
facility → fulfilment facility + understanding + relationship → fulfilment
Q4Apr 20268MCO1 K3

(OR) Critically examine the prevailing notions of happiness and prosperity in society. Mention the consequences.

Answer

Prevailing notion of happiness: happiness = pleasure from sense inputs: eating, entertainment, shopping, more comfort. It is momentary and depends on external things. Prevailing notion of prosperity: prosperity = accumulation of money and goods, "more and more" with no limit, measured by comparison with others.

Critical examination: (1) pleasure from the senses is short-lived and fades, which makes us seek more; (2) happiness is a state in 'I', not in objects; (3) the need for physical facility is finite, but accumulation is unlimited, so the feeling of "enough" never arrives; (4) the real aspirations are continuous happiness and the feeling of having more than required, which come from right understanding and relationship.

Consequences: individual: stress, anxiety, depression, suicides; family: mistrust, divorce, generation gap; society: terrorism, communalism, corruption, wars, inequality; nature: pollution, global warming, resource depletion, loss of species. The common cause is the wrong understanding, which keeps us in animal consciousness (living for facility only) instead of human consciousness.
