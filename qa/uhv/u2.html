Harmony in the Human Being: Harmony in Myself

Universal Human Values II · 4 questions · Notes for this unit

Q1Apr 20268MCO2 K4

Differentiate between the needs and activities of Self and body.

Answer
Self ('I') Body
Nature conscious (sentient), sees, knows, feels material (physical-chemical) with bio parts, non-conscious
Needs Happiness (continuous), through right understanding, trust, respect...; qualitative Physical facility (food, clothing, shelter, medicine...); quantitative, limited
Activities Desire, thought, expectation (imagination), selection, realisation, understanding; also assuming, knowing, recognising, fulfilling Eating, breathing, digestion, moving, sensing, doing; recognising and fulfilling (mechanical)
Satisfaction Happiness or unhappiness in 'I' Pleasant or unpleasant sensation
Role doer, seer, enjoyer instrument

Mistake: treating the needs of the 'I' as if the Body could meet them, or the other way round (confusing "I" with the Body).

Q2Apr 20268MCO2 K2

Body is an instrument of 'I'. Justify the statement.

Answer
  • 'I' is the doer, seer and enjoyer; it decides (desire, thought, selection), sees/understands and experiences the result. The Body carries out the decisions: the hands write, the legs walk, the eyes see for 'I'. Without 'I' the body is lifeless (at death it no longer responds). So the 'I' is the master, and the Body a tool/instrument.
  • Example: a driver and a car; the driver decides and the car carries. The writer and the pen; the 'I' that wants to write uses the hand.
  • Observation: I can say "my body", "my hand"; the one who says my is different from what is called my. My feelings (anger, joy) are not in the body, while physical things (hunger, pain) are felt by 'I' through it.
  • The relationship is 'I' controlling the body (Sanyam) and the body keeping healthy to act as per 'I'. If instead the body's cravings drive the 'I' (junk food, sleeping late), the instrument controls the master and both suffer.
Q3Apr 20268MCO2 K2

(OR) Explain: "Consequences of imagination are self-organization or enslavement".

Answer

Imagination = the activity of 'I' made of desire, thought, expectation and selection (the "mind").

  • When imagination runs as per natural acceptance (the 'I' is aware of what it really wants and decides), it leads to self-organisation: the 'I' is in control, thoughts are in line with right understanding, behaviour and work become harmonious, and the Body is used rightly (Sanyam). The result is continuous happiness.
  • When imagination is guided by pre-conditioning, sense pleasures, the opinions of others, fashion, advertisements, it enslaves the 'I': it is dragged by external things and by the body's cravings, becomes restless, dependent and in conflict (contradiction between what I want to be and what I do). The result is unhappiness.
  • So the same faculty gives either freedom or bondage; right understanding decides which. This is why we need to verify our imagination with natural acceptance, rather than live by liking and disliking.
Q4Apr 20268MCO2 K2

(OR) Mention the three aspects involved in the "programme for self-regulation and health". Explain briefly.

Answer

(⚠ Check the exact "three aspects" with your faculty notes: the standard textbook programme has Intake, Routine and Exercise as the preventive aspects, with Treatment as the corrective fourth.) Health = the body parts in harmony, acting as per 'I'. Sanyam = responsibility in 'I' for nurturing, protecting and rightly utilising the body. The programme has:

  1. Intake (Aahar): what, how much, and when we eat, drink and breathe; healthy, right quantity, clean air and water. Food should nurture the body and not just please the tongue.
  2. Routine (Vihar): right balance of work, rest, sleep and recreation, regular timing.
  3. Physical exercise (Asan, Pranayam): keeps the parts of the body in harmony and responsive to the 'I'.
  4. (Treatment, Aushadhi): medicine only when a disease occurs; it deals with disease but does not ensure health. Health needs the continuing programme above.
