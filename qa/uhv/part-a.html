Part A

Universal Human Values II: short answer questions

2 marks each · 21 questions

Wording of some UHV answers depends on the faculty's own notes. Items marked with a warning sign should be checked against them.

Q1Apr 20262MCO1 K1

Define the following terms: (i) Skill education (ii) Value education.

Answer

(i) Skill education develops the competence to do something: technical, professional or managerial ability (engineering, programming, accounting). It answers "how to do it". (ii) Value education gives the right understanding of what is valuable for the human being so that we know what to do and why, and live with it naturally. It answers "what is worth doing" and gives direction to the skills. Both are needed: skills without values are misused, values without skills cannot be implemented.

Q2Apr 20262MCO1 K1

Differentiate between self-exploration and natural acceptance.

Answer
Self-exploration Natural acceptance
The process of investigating, within myself, what is right The inner acceptance (feeling in 'I') used as the reference in that process
Done with effort: verify each proposal on my own right Not made, always present: from within, constant, the same in all humans
Needs both natural acceptance and experiential validation Does not change with time, place or person
Q3Apr 20262MCO2 K1

Identify the two common responses of both Self and body: Assuming, Recognizing, Fulfilling, Knowing.

Answer

Recognizing and Fulfilling. Assuming and Knowing are the responses of the Self ('I') alone. (The body also recognises, e.g. the tongue tells sweet from sour, and fulfils, e.g. carries out the action, but without understanding.)

Q4Apr 20262MCO2 K1

A boy wants to eat a sweet. Who wants to eat — Self or body? The boy feels the taste. Who feels the taste — Self or body?

Answer

The wanting (desire) is in the Self ('I'). The taste is felt by the Self: the body (tongue) only gives the sensation; the feeling/experience of sweetness happens in 'I'. (The body, in contrast, needs food in a limited quantity to nurture itself.)

Q5Apr 20262MCO3 K1

"Special seating is arranged for two celebrities in the first row in a marriage function, as an expression of respect." Do you agree according to UHV? Justify.

Answer

No. Respect is right evaluation of a person as he is, and it applies to everyone alike. Here the treatment differs on the basis of fame/position (physical-facility-based) differentiation; the others are implicitly under-evaluated. This is differentiation, not respect. It may be a courtesy or protocol, but UHV respect needs to be given to all guests equally.

Q6Apr 20262MCO3 K1

Differentiate between competence and intention.

Answer

Intention = what a person wants (natural acceptance): to be happy and to make others happy. It is good in all. Competence = the ability to fulfil that intention, which is acquired and varies. Trust is on intention (assume it is right); we help to improve the competence. Mistake: judging a person's intention by his competence.

Q7Apr 20262MCO4 K1

Air indicates the _ order, while Rose indicates the ___ order existing in Nature.

Answer

Air indicates the material (physical/padartha) order, while Rose indicates the bio (pran) order.

Q8Apr 20262MCO4 K1

Among the four orders of Nature, identify the order that has breed-based inheritance. Give an example of a unit in that order.

Answer

The animal order (jeev avastha) has breed-based (vansh) inheritance: behaviour is fixed by the breed. Example: a cow, tiger, dog or sparrow. (Bio order: seed-based; human order: sanskar-based, i.e. through values and understanding.)

Q9Apr 20262MCO5 K1

Explain any two criteria for evaluation of a management model.

Answer

(Check the wording with your faculty notes.)

  1. Does it ensure mutual fulfilment? The model should fulfil the needs of all: the workers, the customers, the society and nature, not only the profit of the owner.
  2. Is it based on universal human values? It should be built on trust, respect, cooperation and justice rather than competition and exploitation, and use resources in a sustainable way.
  3. (Also asked) Is it practicable and replicable, and does it develop the competence of people?
Q10Apr 20262MCO5 K1

Identify the consequence of the misunderstanding "Professional excellence is wealth generation".

Answer

It makes profit the only aim of professional work. Consequences: exploitation of workers, customers and nature; unethical practices and corruption; products designed for sales (obsolescence) rather than need; loss of trust and increasing inequality, stress and conflict. Correct view: professional excellence is using one's competence for the mutual fulfilment of all (the universal human order); wealth is a by-product and not the goal.

Q11Expected2MCO1 K1

What are the basic human aspirations?

Answer

Continuous happiness and prosperity for oneself and for all. Fulfilled by Right understanding, Relationship and Physical facility (in that priority).

Q12Expected2MCO1 K1

State the correct priority of the three requirements for fulfilment of the aspirations.

Answer

Right understanding (1) → Relationship (2) → Physical facility (3).

Q13Expected2MCO2 K1

What is Sanyam? What is Health?

Answer

Sanyam (self-regulation) = the feeling of responsibility in 'I' for the nurturing, protection and right utilisation of the Body. Health = the Body's parts working in harmony and acting as per the 'I'.

Q14Expected2MCO2 K1

List the activities of the Self ('I').

Answer

Desire, thought, expectation, selection (chayan) (these four are imagination) and realisation and understanding.

Q15Expected2MCO3 K1

List the nine values in a relationship.

Answer

Trust, Respect, Affection, Care, Guidance, Reverence, Glory, Gratitude, Love.

Q16Expected2MCO3 K1

List the four comprehensive human goals.

Answer

Resolution (samadhan), Prosperity (samriddhi), Fearlessness (abhay), Co-existence (sah-astitva).

Q17Expected2MCO3 K1

List the five dimensions of the human order.

Answer

Education–Right living; Health–Self-regulation; Justice–Preservation; Production–Work; Exchange–Storage.

Q18Expected2MCO4 K1

List the four orders of Nature.

Answer

Material (padartha), Bio (pran), Animal (jeev) and Human (gyan) orders.

Q19Expected2MCO4 K1

What are recyclability and self-regulation in Nature?

Answer

Recyclability: matter and energy circulate in nature (the waste of one unit is the input for another). Self-regulation: each order and unit maintains its balance in quantity and relationship on its own.

Q20Expected2MCO5 K1

What is meant by ethical human conduct?

Answer

Conduct based on the right understanding of universal human values, with definite, universal basis (values → policy → character), not on law, custom or personal liking alone.

Q21Expected2MCO5 K1

List the three abilities that make up competence in professional ethics.

Answer

(1) Utilising professional competence for augmenting the universal human order; (2) ability to identify the scope and characteristics of people-friendly and eco-friendly production systems; (3) ability to identify and develop appropriate technologies and management patterns for these systems.
