Combinational Circuits and Programmable Logic Devices

Digital Logic Design · 5 questions · Notes for this unit

Q1Expected8MCO3 K3

Explain the 4-bit binary parallel adder with carry look-ahead.

Answer

A ripple-carry adder has 4 full adders in a chain; each carry waits for the previous one, so the delay grows with the number of bits.

Carry look-ahead: define for each bit generate \(G_i=A_iB_i\) and propagate \(P_i=A_i\oplus B_i\). Then \(S_i=P_i\oplus C_i\), \(C_{i+1}=G_i+P_iC_i\). Expanding (all carries depend only on inputs and \(C_0\)): \[C_1=G_0+P_0C_0\] \[C_2=G_1+P_1G_0+P_1P_0C_0\] \[C_3=G_2+P_2G_1+P_2P_1G_0+P_2P_1P_0C_0\] \[C_4=G_3+P_3G_2+P_3P_2G_1+P_3P_2P_1G_0+P_3P_2P_1P_0C_0\] All carries are produced in two gate levels after \(G,P\) (constant delay), at the cost of more gates. Subtraction: feed \(B\) through XOR gates controlled by a mode input \(M\) and set \(C_0=M\) (2's complement).

Q2Expected8MCO3 K3

Design a BCD adder.

Answer

Adds two BCD digits and gives a BCD digit plus carry. Steps: (1) add the two 4-bit numbers with a 4-bit binary adder, giving \(K,Z_8Z_4Z_2Z_1\) (the intermediate sum, 0 to 19); (2) if the sum is greater than 9 or has a carry, add 0110 (6) to correct it. Correction condition: \(C=K+Z_8Z_4+Z_8Z_2\) (the sum is 10 to 15 when \(Z_8=1\) and either \(Z_4\) or \(Z_2\) is 1; the carry \(K\) covers 16 to 19). Example: \(8+7\): binary sum \(1111\); \(Z_8Z_4=1\), so add \(0110\): \(1111+0110=1\,0101\) → carry 1 and digit 5, i.e. BCD \(15\) ✓.

Q3Expected8MCO3 K3

Implement a full adder using a 3-to-8 decoder, and implement \(F(A,B,C)=\sum m(1,2,6,7)\) using a 4:1 multiplexer.

Answer

Full adder with a decoder: a 3-to-8 decoder with inputs \(A,B,C_{in}\) gives all minterms \(m_0\ldots m_7\). Sum \(=\sum m(1,2,4,7)\) → one 4-input OR gate on outputs \(m_1,m_2,m_4,m_7\). Carry \(=\sum m(3,5,6,7)\) → one 4-input OR gate on outputs \(m_3,m_5,m_6,m_7\).

4:1 MUX for \(F=\sum m(1,2,6,7)\): use \(A,B\) as the select lines (\(S_1=A,S_0=B\)) and \(C\) for the data inputs.

AB minterms F in terms of C Data input
00 \(m_0,m_1\) \(m_1\) present → \(C\) \(I_0=C\)
01 \(m_2,m_3\) \(m_2\) present → \(C'\) \(I_1=C'\)
10 \(m_4,m_5\) none \(I_2=0\)
11 \(m_6,m_7\) both → 1 \(I_3=1\)
Q4Expected8MCO3 K2

Design a 2-bit magnitude comparator.

Answer

Inputs \(A_1A_0\) and \(B_1B_0\); outputs \(A>B\), \(A=B\), \(A<B\). Let \(x_i=A_i\odot B_i\) (equal bits). \[(A=B)=x_1x_0\] \[(A>B)=A_1B_1'+x_1A_0B_0'\] \[(A<B)=A_1'B_1+x_1A_0'B_0\] The idea: compare the most significant bits first; if they are equal compare the next. Check \(A=10,B=01\): \(A_1B_1'=1\cdot1=1\) → \(A>B\) ✓.

Q5Expected8MCO3 K2

Compare PROM, PLA and PAL. Show how a PLA implements \(F_1=AB'+AC+A'BC'\) and \(F_2=AC+BC\).

Answer
Device AND array OR array Notes
PROM fixed (full decoder) programmable all \(2^n\) minterms available; inefficient for few terms
PAL programmable fixed cheaper, easy; limited sharing of product terms
PLA programmable programmable most flexible; product terms shared between outputs

PLA programming table (product terms and outputs):

Product term A B C F1 F2
1. \(AB'\) 1 0 – 1 –
2. \(AC\) 1 – 1 1 1
3. \(BC\) – 1 1 – 1
4. \(A'BC'\) 0 1 0 1 –

\(F_1=\) term 1 + term 2 + term 4; \(F_2=\) term 2 + term 3. Term 2 (\(AC\)) is shared by both outputs: that is the PLA advantage. Only 4 product terms are needed. FPGA (introduction): an array of configurable logic blocks (each with look-up tables and flip-flops), I/O blocks and programmable interconnect, configured by loading a bitstream into SRAM cells. It is reprogrammable and can implement large sequential and combinational designs.
