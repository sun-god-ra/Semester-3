Non-Parametric Statistical Tests

Probability and Statistics · 5 questions · Notes for this unit

Q1Nov 20258MCO5 K3

A company claims that the median assembly time of a product is 50 minutes. A sample of 12 workers gave 52, 48, 49, 55, 51, 47, 50, 53, 46, 54, 49, 51. Use the sign test at the 5% level.

Answer

\(H_0:\tilde\mu=50\), \(H_1:\tilde\mu\ne50\). Signs: 52(+), 48(−), 49(−), 55(+), 51(+), 47(−), 50 (tie, discard), 53(+), 46(−), 54(+), 49(−), 51(+). Plus signs \(=6\), minus signs \(=5\), so effective \(n=11\). Under \(H_0\): \(X\sim B(11,0.5)\). Test statistic = smaller count \(=5\). \(P(X\le5)=0.5\) (symmetry of \(B(11,\frac12)\)), so the two-sided \(p=2(0.5)=1.0\) (capped at 1). \(p=1.0>0.05\): cannot reject \(H_0\). The sample gives no evidence against the claim of a 50-minute median.

Q2Nov 20258MCO5 K3

The following arrangement indicates whether 60 consecutive cars passing a toll booth had local plates (L) or out-of-state plates (O). Test at the 0.05 level whether the arrangement of L's and O's may be regarded as random.

Answer

Count the symbols from the paper: \(n_1=38\) (L), \(n_2=22\) (O), number of runs \(u=28\). (These counts come from my reading of the scanned sequence; recount on your paper and put in your own numbers using the same formulas.)

\(H_0\): the arrangement is random. \(H_1\): not random. Both \(n_1,n_2\) are large, so use the normal approximation: \[\mu_u=\frac{2n_1n_2}{n_1+n_2}+1=\frac{2(38)(22)}{60}+1=28.87\] \[\sigma_u=\sqrt{\frac{2n_1n_2(2n_1n_2-n_1-n_2)}{(n_1+n_2)^2(n_1+n_2-1)}}=\sqrt{\frac{1672(1612)}{3600(59)}}=3.56\] \[z=\frac{u-\mu_u}{\sigma_u}=\frac{28-28.87}{3.56}=-0.24\] \(|z|=0.24<1.96\): do not reject \(H_0\); the arrangement may be regarded as random.

Q3Nov 20258MCO5 K2

(OR) Explain the steps involved in the Mann–Whitney \(U\) test.

Answer

Used to test whether two independent samples come from identical populations (non-parametric alternative to the two-sample \(t\) test).

  1. State \(H_0\): the two populations are identical; \(H_1\): one tends to give larger values (or just "not identical").
  2. Combine the \(n_1+n_2\) values and rank them from smallest (rank 1); tied values get the average of their ranks.
  3. Find \(W_1\) and \(W_2\), the rank sums of sample 1 and sample 2 (check \(W_1+W_2=\frac{N(N+1)}2\), \(N=n_1+n_2\)).
  4. Compute \(U_1=W_1-\dfrac{n_1(n_1+1)}2\) and \(U_2=W_2-\dfrac{n_2(n_2+1)}2\); check \(U_1+U_2=n_1n_2\).
  5. For small samples compare \(\min(U_1,U_2)\) with the table value. For large samples (\(n_1,n_2>8\)) use the normal approximation: \(\mu_U=\dfrac{n_1n_2}2\), \(\sigma_U^2=\dfrac{n_1n_2(n_1+n_2+1)}{12}\), \(Z=\dfrac{U_1-\mu_U}{\sigma_U}\).
  6. Decide: reject \(H_0\) if \(|Z|>z_{\alpha/2}\) (two-sided), or \(Z>z_\alpha\) / \(Z<-z_\alpha\) for one-sided tests. Worked example from the lectures: two sands, \(n_1=15,n_2=14\): \(W_1=162\), \(U_1=42\), \(Z=-2.75<-2.575\), reject at 1%.
Q4Nov 20258MCO5 K3

(OR) The ranks assigned by two judges to 8 contestants: Judge A: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8; Judge B: 2, 1, 4, 3, 5, 7, 6, 8. Compute Spearman's rank correlation and test for significance.

Answer
A 1 2 3 4 5 6 7 8
B 2 1 4 3 5 7 6 8
\(d\) −1 1 −1 1 0 −1 1 0
\(d^2\) 1 1 1 1 0 1 1 0

\(\sum d^2=6\). \[r_s=1-\frac{6\sum d^2}{n(n^2-1)}=1-\frac{36}{8\times63}=1-0.0714=\mathbf{0.9286}\] Test: \(H_0\): no association (\(\rho_s=0\)); \(H_1\): association exists.

  • Using the lecturer's statistic \(Z=r_s\sqrt n=0.9286\times2.828=2.63>1.96\): reject at 5%.
  • Using the common book form \(z=r_s\sqrt{n-1}=0.9286\times2.646=2.46>1.96\): also reject.
  • (For small \(n=8\) the exact table critical value at 5% two-tailed is \(0.738\); \(0.9286>0.738\) also gives significance.) Conclusion: the two judges' rankings are strongly and significantly positively related.
Q5Expected8MCO5 K3

Explain the Kruskal–Wallis test with an example.

Answer

Non-parametric alternative to one-way ANOVA for \(k\ge3\) independent samples. Rank all \(n\) observations together, find the rank sum \(R_i\) of each group. \[H=\frac{12}{n(n+1)}\sum_{i=1}^k\frac{R_i^2}{n_i}-3(n+1)\] Valid if every \(n_i>5\); compare \(H\) with \(\chi^2_\alpha(k-1)\). Reject if \(H>\chi^2_\alpha\). Example (corrosion methods A, B, C; \(n=18\)): \(R_A=84,R_B=55.5,R_C=31.5\), \(H=\dfrac{12}{18\cdot19}\left(\dfrac{84^2}6+\dfrac{55.5^2}7+\dfrac{31.5^2}5\right)-57=6.67>5.991=\chi^2_{0.05}(2)\): reject; the three methods are not equally effective.
