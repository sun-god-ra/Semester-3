Part B · Unit IV

Variances and Proportions

Probability and Statistics · 4 questions · Notes for this unit

Q1Nov 202516MCO4 K4

Tests of the fidelity and selectivity of 190 digital radio receivers gave the following table. Use the 0.01 level to test whether there is a dependence between fidelity and selectivity.

Answer
Selectivity \ Fidelity Low Average High Total
Low 6 12 32 50
Average 33 61 18 112
High 13 15 0 28
Total 52 88 50 190

\(H_0\): fidelity and selectivity are independent. \(H_1\): they are dependent. \(\alpha=0.01\), d.f. \(=(3-1)(3-1)=4\), \(\chi^2_{0.01}(4)=13.277\).

Expected \(E=\dfrac{(\text{row total})(\text{column total})}{190}\):

\(E\) Low Average High
Low 13.68 23.16 13.16
Average 30.65 51.87 29.47
High 7.66 12.97 7.37

Contributions \((O-E)^2/E\):

Low Average High
Low 4.31 5.38 26.98
Average 0.18 1.61 4.47
High 3.72 0.32 7.37

\(\chi^2=\sum\dfrac{(O-E)^2}{E}=\mathbf{54.33}\) (sum of the nine values). \(54.33>13.277\): reject \(H_0\). There is a dependence between fidelity and selectivity at the 1% level. (The largest contribution is the Low–High cell, 26.98: far more receivers with low selectivity have high fidelity than independence predicts.) All expected frequencies are at least 5, so the test is valid.

Q2Nov 20258MCO4 K4

(OR) In a sample of 1000 people in Karnataka, 540 are rice eaters and the rest wheat eaters. Test, at the 1% level, the hypothesis that rice and wheat are equally popular.

Answer

\(H_0:p=0.5\), \(H_1:p\ne0.5\), \(\hat p=0.54\), \(n=1000\), \(\alpha=0.01\): critical \(|z|=2.575\). \[z=\frac{\hat p-p_0}{\sqrt{p_0q_0/n}}=\frac{0.54-0.5}{\sqrt{0.25/1000}}=\frac{0.04}{0.01581}=2.53\] \(2.53<2.575\): do not reject \(H_0\). At the 1% level rice and wheat are equally popular (it would be significant at 5%, where the limit is 1.96).

Q3Nov 20258MCO4 K4

(OR) In a sample of 8 observations the sum of squares of deviations from the sample mean was 94.5, and in another sample of 10 observations it was 101.7. Test whether the difference of variances is significant (at \(\alpha=5\%\), \(F\)-table value 3.29).

Answer

\(H_0:\sigma_1^2=\sigma_2^2\). \(s_1^2=\dfrac{94.5}{8-1}=13.5\), \(s_2^2=\dfrac{101.7}{10-1}=11.3\). \[F=\frac{s_1^2}{s_2^2}=\frac{13.5}{11.3}=1.1947\] d.f. \(=(7,9)\), table value \(=3.29\). \(1.1947<3.29\): do not reject \(H_0\). The difference between the variances is not significant.

Q4Expected8MCO4 K3

A die is thrown 120 times with outcomes 1 to 6 occurring 25, 17, 15, 23, 24, 16 times. Test at 5% whether the die is fair.

Answer

\(H_0\): fair die. Expected frequency \(=120/6=20\) each. \[\chi^2=\sum\frac{(O-E)^2}{E}=\frac{25+9+25+9+16+16}{20}=\frac{100}{20}=5.0\] d.f. \(=6-1=5\), \(\chi^2_{0.05}(5)=11.07\). \(5.0<11.07\): do not reject \(H_0\); the die may be taken as fair.
