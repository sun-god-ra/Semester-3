Part B · Unit III

Statistical Inference

Probability and Statistics · 5 questions · Notes for this unit

Q1Nov 20258MCO3 K3

A sample of 400 items is taken from a population whose standard deviation is 10. The sample mean is 40. Test whether the sample has come from a population with mean 38. Also calculate the 95% confidence interval for the population mean.

Answer

\(H_0:\mu=38\), \(H_1:\mu\ne38\). Large sample, \(\sigma\) known, \(\alpha=0.05\): critical \(|z|=1.96\).

\[z=\frac{\bar x-\mu_0}{\sigma/\sqrt n}=\frac{40-38}{10/\sqrt{400}}=\frac{2}{0.5}=4\] \(|z|=4>1.96\): reject \(H_0\). The sample has not come from a population with mean 38.

95% CI: \(\bar x\pm1.96\dfrac\sigma{\sqrt n}=40\pm1.96(0.5)=40\pm0.98=\mathbf{(39.02,\ 40.98)}\). Since 38 is outside the interval, the conclusion agrees.

Q2Nov 20258MCO3 K2

Explain the procedure for testing of hypothesis.

Answer
  1. State \(H_0\) (equality) and the alternative \(H_1\) (decides one- or two-tailed).
  2. Choose the level of significance \(\alpha\) (usually 5% or 1%).
  3. Choose the test statistic and its sampling distribution: \(z\) (large sample or \(\sigma\) known), \(t\) (small sample, \(\sigma\) unknown), \(\chi^2\), \(F\).
  4. Find the critical region (critical value) from the tables for the chosen \(\alpha\) and tail.
  5. Compute the test statistic from the sample.
  6. Decide: if the value falls in the critical region (or \(p\le\alpha\)), reject \(H_0\); else do not reject (accept) \(H_0\).
  7. Conclude in words about the problem.

Errors: Type I (\(\alpha\)) and Type II (\(\beta\)); "accepting \(H_0\)" means only that the evidence is not strong enough to reject it.

Q3Nov 20258MCO3 K2

A research worker wants to determine the average time a mechanic takes to rotate the tires of a car, and wants to assert with 95% confidence that her sample mean is off by at most 0.50 min. If \(\sigma=1.6\) min, how large a sample must she take?

Answer

Sample size: \(n=\left(\dfrac{z_{\alpha/2}\,\sigma}{E}\right)^2=\left(\dfrac{1.96\times1.6}{0.5}\right)^2=(6.272)^2=39.34\). Always round up: \(n=\mathbf{40}\).

Q4Nov 20258MCO3 K3

The following are the average weekly losses of worker-hours due to accidents in 10 industrial plants before and after a safety programme: before 45, 73, 46, 124, 33, 57, 83, 34, 26, 17; after 36, 60, 44, 119, 35, 51, 77, 29, 24, 11. Use the 0.05 level to test whether the safety programme is effective.

Answer

Paired data, small sample: paired \(t\)-test. \(D=\) before − after: 9, 13, 2, 5, −2, 6, 6, 5, 2, 6. \(H_0:\mu_D=0\), \(H_1:\mu_D>0\) (programme reduces losses), \(\alpha=0.05\) one-tailed, d.f. \(=9\), \(t_{0.05}(9)=1.833\).

\(\bar D=\dfrac{52}{10}=5.2\), \(s_D=4.08\) (from \(\sum D^2=420\), so \(s_D^2=\frac{420-10(5.2)^2}{9}=\frac{149.6}{9}=16.62\)). \[t=\frac{\bar D}{s_D/\sqrt n}=\frac{5.2}{4.08/\sqrt{10}}=4.03>1.833\] Reject \(H_0\): the safety programme is effective. (The sign test and Spearman test on the same data also agree: see Unit V.)

Q5Expected8MCO3 K3

Two groups have \(n_1=10,\bar x_1=52,s_1=4\) and \(n_2=12,\bar x_2=48,s_2=5\). Test at 5% whether the means differ (assume equal population variances).

Answer

\(H_0:\mu_1=\mu_2\), \(H_1:\mu_1\ne\mu_2\). Pooled variance: \(s_p^2=\dfrac{9(16)+11(25)}{20}=20.95\), \(s_p=4.577\). \[t=\frac{52-48}{4.577\sqrt{\frac1{10}+\frac1{12}}}=\frac{4}{1.960}=2.04\] d.f. \(=20\), \(t_{0.025}(20)=2.086\). \(2.04<2.086\): do not reject \(H_0\) at 5% (a narrow miss; it would be significant at the 10% level).
