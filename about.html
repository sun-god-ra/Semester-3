About this site

How it is organised

  • Notes: subject → unit → topic. Each topic page has short notebook-style notes and, where written, a separate explanation.
  • Questions: Part A (2 marks) and Part B (8 marks) for each subject, modelled on the SAHE papers. Past-paper questions are tagged with their exam month, predicted ones are tagged Expected.
  • Terms: every underlined word opens an internal page for that term with excerpts from the notes and a list of the pages where it is used.
  • Search: client-side, works offline.

What was and was not verified

  • The notes are written from subject knowledge against the course-book syllabus. They are not page-verified against the textbooks.
  • Probability and Statistics follows the lecture slides supplied (Dr M. V. Rao). Slips found in the slides are flagged in the notes.
  • All Java programs in the questions section were compiled and run, except the two-file package example, which needs both files. C programs in the OS lab were compiled and run. PowerShell and batch scripts and OS lab Task 2 were not executed.
  • Numerical answers in the P&S and OS questions were recomputed with Python.
  • No Digital Logic Design past paper was found, so its questions are style-derived practice questions.
  • Some UHV answers depend on faculty wording: the "four steps of understanding harmony", the "three aspects of the self-regulation programme" and "innateness". Check them against your class notes.
  • FPGA pin assignments in the DLD lab are placeholders and IC pin-outs are from memory. Check the datasheet before wiring.
  • The term pages show excerpts from the notes; they are not independent encyclopedia articles.

Using it offline

Open index.html in any modern browser. No server and no internet connection are needed. Maths (MathJax), diagrams (Mermaid) and fonts are bundled in the assets folder.
