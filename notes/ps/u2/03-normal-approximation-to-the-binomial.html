Normal Approximation to the Binomial

Probability and Statistics · Unit II: Probability Densities and Joint Distributions

Normal Approximation to the Binomial
Subject
Probability and Statistics (24MA203)
Unit
Unit II: Probability Densities and Joint Distributions
Topic
3 of 7
Terms
Continuity correction, Standard deviation

Practice: Part B questions for Unit II and Part A short questions.

Notebook notes
  • For large \(n\), binomial \(b(x;n,p)\) ≈ normal with

\[\mu=np,\qquad \sigma=\sqrt{npq}\]

  • Condition (as taught in the lecture slides): \(np>15\) and \(n(1-p)>15\). Other books use \(np\ge5\) and \(nq\ge5\) or \(\ge10\); write the lecture condition in the exam, and mention the other if space permits.
  • Binomial is discrete, normal is continuous → use continuity correction (±0.5)
Want Use
\(P(X=a)\) \(P(a-0.5<X<a+0.5)\)
\(P(X\le a)\) \(P(X<a+0.5)\)
\(P(X<a)\) \(P(X<a-0.5)\)
\(P(X\ge a)\) \(P(X>a-0.5)\)
\(P(X>a)\) \(P(X>a+0.5)\)
\(P(a\le X\le b)\) \(P(a-0.5<X<b+0.5)\)
  • Trick: for "≤" and "≥" the number itself is included so extend by 0.5 outward.

Example: \(n=100,\ p=0.2\) → \(\mu=20,\ \sigma=4\)

  • \(P(X\le15)\approx P\big(Z\le\frac{15.5-20}{4}\big)=\Phi(-1.125)=\mathbf{0.1303}\)
  • \(P(X=15)\approx\Phi(-1.125)-\Phi(-1.375)=0.1303-0.0846=\mathbf{0.0457}\)
Explanation

When \(n\) is large, calculating a binomial probability needs many terms. The shape of the binomial bars becomes bell-like as \(n\) grows, and a normal curve with the same mean \(np\) and standard deviation \(\sqrt{npq}\) fits it well. The condition (\(np>15\) and \(n(1-p)>15\) in the lecture slides; looser rules of thumb like 5 or 10 appear in other books) makes sure the curve is not squashed against the wall at 0 or \(n\).

Each binomial bar is centred on a whole number \(a\) and is one unit wide, so it covers the interval from \(a-0.5\) to \(a+0.5\) on the continuous scale. The continuity correction simply respects that width. For \(P(X\le15)\) the bar for 15 must be fully included, so the boundary is 15.5. A quick check is to sketch the bars and shade the ones you want.
