Notebook notes
7a. Continuous random variables

Q. \(f(x)=kx\) for \(0\le x\le2\).

  • \(\int_0^2kx\,dx=2k=1\Rightarrow k=\frac12\)
  • \(F(0.5)=\int_0^{0.5}\frac x2dx=\frac{0.25}4=\mathbf{0.0625}\)
  • \(P(1\le X\le1.5)=\frac{1.5^2-1^2}{4}=\mathbf{0.3125}\)

Q. \(f(x)=2e^{-2x}\), \(x>0\). Probability between 1 and 3, above 0.5, cdf, mean, variance.

  • \(P(1\le X\le3)=e^{-2}-e^{-6}=\mathbf{0.1329}\)
  • \(P(X\ge0.5)=e^{-1}=\mathbf{0.3679}\)
  • \(F(x)=1-e^{-2x}\) for \(x>0\), else 0
  • Mean \(=\frac12\), Var \(=\frac14\), \(\sigma=\frac12\)

Q. Find \(k\), mean, variance. (i) \(f(x)=\dfrac k{1+x^2}\) on \((-\infty,\infty)\); (ii) \(f(x)=kx^3\) on \((0,1)\).

  • (i) \(\int\frac{dx}{1+x^2}=\pi\), so \(k=\frac1\pi\). This is the Cauchy density. Its mean does not exist (\(\int|x|f\) diverges) and so neither does its variance. If the question asks for mean and variance, the correct answer is "does not exist". (The graph is symmetric about 0, but symmetry alone is not enough for a mean to exist.)
  • (ii) \(\frac k4=1\Rightarrow k=4\); mean \(=4\int_0^1x^4dx=\frac45\); \(E(X^2)=4\int_0^1x^5dx=\frac23\); Var \(=\frac23-\frac{16}{25}=\mathbf{0.0267}\)

7c. Normal-distribution word problems

Cosmic radiation (\(\mu=4.35,\ \sigma=0.59\) mrem)

  • (a) between 4.00 and 5.00: \(z=-0.59\) to \(1.10\), probability \(\mathbf{0.5882}\)
  • (b) at least 5.50: \(z=1.95\), probability \(\mathbf{0.0256}\)

Bar-code scanners (\(\mu=10.1,\ \sigma=2.7\) dB)

  • (a) between 8.5 and 13.0: \(z=-0.59\) to \(1.07\), probability \(\mathbf{0.5819}\) (the same as the proportion of products in that range, part (b) asks the same thing in "proportion" words)
  • (c) greater than 15.1: \(z=1.85\), probability \(\mathbf{0.0320}\)

Coffee jars (\(\sigma=0.04\) oz). Only 2% of jars may hold less than 4 oz, so \(P(X<4)=0.02\).

  • \(z=-2.054\) ⇒ \(\frac{4-\mu}{0.04}=-2.054\Rightarrow\mu=4+2.054(0.04)=\mathbf{4.082}\) oz

Gold assays. \(\ln X\sim N(-4.6,\ \sigma^2=1.21)\) so \(\sigma=1.1\). Is \(X\le0.0015\) unusual?

  • \(\ln0.0015=-6.502\), \(z=\frac{-6.502+4.6}{1.1}=-1.73\), \(P=\mathbf{0.0419}\)
  • About 4% — fairly unusual, but not extremely rare (most books use 5% or less as the "unusual" line; this just falls below it).

Power outages (\(\mu=11.6,\ \sigma=3.3\) per month). At least 8 outages.

  • Outages are whole numbers, so "at least 8" starts at \(7.5\) (continuity correction): \(z=\frac{7.5-11.6}{3.3}=-1.24\)
  • \(P(X\ge8)=1-0.1070=\mathbf{0.893}\)
7d. Normal approximation to binomial (memory chips)

20% of chips are defective, a lot of 100 is inspected. \(\mu=np=20,\ \sigma=\sqrt{100(0.2)(0.8)}=4\). Check the condition: \(np=20>15\) and \(n(1-p)=80>15\) ✓.

  • (a) exactly 15: \(P(14.5<X<15.5)=\Phi(-1.125)-\Phi(-1.375)=0.1303-0.0846=\mathbf{0.0457}\) (exact binomial 0.0481)
  • (b) at most 15: \(P(X<15.5)=\Phi(-1.125)=\mathbf{0.1303}\) (exact binomial 0.1285)
7e. Joint distributions

Discrete table.

\(x_1=0\) \(x_1=1\) \(x_1=2\) \(f_2(x_2)\)
\(x_2=0\) 0.1 0.4 0.1 0.6
\(x_2=1\) 0.2 0.2 0 0.4
\(f_1(x_1)\) 0.3 0.6 0.1 1.0
  • \(P(X_1+X_2>1)=f(1,1)+f(2,0)+f(2,1)=0.2+0.1+0=\mathbf{0.3}\)
  • Conditional given \(X_2=1\): \(f_1(0|1)=\frac{0.2}{0.4}=0.5,\ f_1(1|1)=0.5,\ f_1(2|1)=0\)
  • Independence test: \(f(0,1)=0.2\) but \(f_1(0)f_2(1)=0.3\times0.4=\mathbf{0.12}\ne0.2\), so not independent.

⚠ Slide slip: the slide writes "\(0.3\times0.4=1.2\)". The product is \(0.12\). The conclusion (not independent) is still right.

Two scanners from five. 2 with electronic defects, 1 with memory defect, 2 good. Two are chosen. \(X_1\) = electronic defects, \(X_2\) = memory defects. Total ways \(\binom52=10\).

  • \(f(x_1,x_2)=\dfrac{\binom2{x_1}\binom1{x_2}\binom2{2-x_1-x_2}}{10}\). This is the table above.
  • Probability of 0 or 1 total defects: \(f(0,0)+f(1,0)+f(0,1)=0.1+0.4+0.2=\mathbf{0.7}\)
  • Marginal of \(X_1\): \(0.3,\ 0.6,\ 0.1\).

Continuous joint. \(f(x_1,x_2)=6e^{-2x_1-3x_2}\), \(x_1,x_2>0\).

  • (i) \(P(1<X_1<2,\ 2<X_2<3)=(e^{-2}-e^{-4})(e^{-6}-e^{-9})=\mathbf{0.0003}\)
  • (ii) \(P(X_1<2,\ X_2>2)=(1-e^{-4})e^{-6}=\mathbf{0.0024}\) (slide gives 0.0025, rounding)
  • (iii) \(F(x_1,x_2)=(1-e^{-2x_1})(1-e^{-3x_2})\); \(F(1,1)=(1-e^{-2})(1-e^{-3})=\mathbf{0.8216}\)

⚠ Slide slip: one line of the slide writes \((1-e^{-2x_1})(1-e^{-2x_2})\); the second exponent must be \(3x_2\), as the next slide has.

  • (iv) Marginals: \(f_1(x_1)=2e^{-2x_1}\), \(f_2(x_2)=3e^{-3x_2}\). The product equals \(f(x_1,x_2)\), so independent.

Conditional density. \(f(x_1,x_2)=\frac13(x_1+2x_2)\), \(0<x_1<1\), \(0<x_2<1\).

  • \(f_2(x_2)=\frac13\left(\frac12+2x_2\right)=\frac{1+4x_2}6\)
  • \(f_1(x_1|x_2)=\dfrac{\frac13(x_1+2x_2)}{\frac{1+4x_2}6}=\dfrac{2x_1+4x_2}{1+4x_2}\)
  • \(f_1(x_1)=\frac13(x_1+1)\) and \(f_2(x_2|x_1)=\dfrac{x_1+2x_2}{x_1+1}\)
Explanation

The patterns repeat. For a density question, first find \(k\) from "total area = 1". For a normal question, convert to \(z\) and use the table. For "at least \(a\)" with a discrete count, use \(a-0.5\) as the boundary. For a joint question, sums over the table give marginals, and independence means every cell equals the product of its marginals (one failure is enough to say "not independent").

Two warnings. The Cauchy density in 7a looks like an ordinary exercise, but its mean and variance do not exist, so computing "\(0\)" for the mean is a classic wrong answer. And in the coffee-jar problem the direction matters: only 2% may be under 4 oz, so 4 oz sits at \(z=-2.054\), below the mean, which is why the mean has to be set above 4.
