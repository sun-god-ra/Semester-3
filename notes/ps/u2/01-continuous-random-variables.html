Continuous Random Variables

Probability and Statistics · Unit II: Probability Densities and Joint Distributions

Continuous Random Variables
Subject
Probability and Statistics (24MA203)
Unit
Unit II: Probability Densities and Joint Distributions
Topic
1 of 7
Terms
Probability density function, Variance, Probability mass function, Cumulative distribution function

Practice: Part B questions for Unit II and Part A short questions.

Notebook notes
  • Continuous RV → takes any value in an interval. Described by a pdf \(f(x)\) (probability density function)
  • Important: \(f(x)\) is NOT a probability. Probability = area under the curve.
  • Rules for valid pdf:
    • \(f(x)\ge0\)
    • \(\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x)\,dx=1\) (use this to find \(k\))
  • \(P(a\le X\le b)=\displaystyle\int_a^b f(x)\,dx\)
  • For continuous RV: \(P(X=c)=0\) → so \(P(a<X<b)=P(a\le X\le b)\) (ends don't matter!)
  • cdf \(F(x)=\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{x}f(t)\,dt\) and \(f(x)=\dfrac{d}{dx}F(x)\)
  • Mean \(\mu=\displaystyle\int x f(x)\,dx\)
  • Variance \(\sigma^2=\displaystyle\int x^2f(x)\,dx-\mu^2\)

Example: \(f(x)=k(1-x^2)\), \(0<x<1\)

  • \(\int_0^1 k(1-x^2)dx=k\left[x-\tfrac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^1=\tfrac{2k}{3}=1\Rightarrow k=\tfrac32\)
  • \(P(X>0.5)=\tfrac32\left[x-\tfrac{x^3}{3}\right]_{0.5}^{1}=\tfrac{5}{16}=0.3125\)

Example (given cdf): \(F(x)=k(x-1)^4\) for \(1<x\le3\)

  • \(F(3)=1\Rightarrow16k=1\Rightarrow k=\tfrac1{16}\)
  • \(f(x)=F'(x)=\tfrac{(x-1)^3}{4}\), \(1<x<3\)
  • Mean = \(\int_1^3 x\frac{(x-1)^3}{4}dx=2.6\) (sub \(u=x-1\))
Explanation

For a continuous variable there are infinitely many possible values, so the chance of hitting any one exact value is zero. We therefore talk about the probability of landing in an interval, and that probability is the area under the density curve over that interval. The density \(f(x)\) itself can even be larger than 1 at some points, as long as the total area is 1. This is the main difference from a probability mass function.

The practical consequence is that you can ignore whether the end points are included. \(P(X<3)\) and \(P(X\le3)\) are the same number. To find the constant in a density, integrate over the whole range, set the result equal to 1 and solve. The cumulative distribution function is the accumulated area up to \(x\), so differentiating it gives the density back. Mean and variance are computed with integrals in the same pattern as the discrete sums: replace \(\sum\) with \(\int\) and \(f(x)\) with \(f(x)\,dx\).
