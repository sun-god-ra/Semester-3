Joint Distributions: Discrete

Probability and Statistics · Unit II: Probability Densities and Joint Distributions

Joint Distributions: Discrete
Subject
Probability and Statistics (24MA203)
Unit
Unit II: Probability Densities and Joint Distributions
Topic
4 of 7
Terms
Covariance, Joint distribution, Marginal distribution, Conditional distribution

Practice: Part B questions for Unit II and Part A short questions.

Notebook notes
  • Two RVs \(X,Y\) together. Joint pmf \(f(x,y)=P(X=x,\ Y=y)\)
  • Rules: \(f(x,y)\ge0\) and \(\sum_x\sum_y f(x,y)=1\)
  • Marginals (add across the other variable):
    • \(f_1(x)=\sum_y f(x,y)\) ← row total
    • \(f_2(y)=\sum_x f(x,y)\) ← column total
  • Conditional: \(f(y\mid x)=\dfrac{f(x,y)}{f_1(x)}\)
  • Independent if \(f(x,y)=f_1(x)\,f_2(y)\) for all pairs (one failing pair ⇒ dependent)
  • \(E[g(X,Y)]=\sum\sum g(x,y)f(x,y)\)
  • Covariance \(\text{Cov}(X,Y)=E(XY)-E(X)E(Y)\) ; independent ⇒ Cov = 0 (reverse NOT always true)

Example: 5 scanners (2 electronic-defect, 1 memory-defect, 2 good). Pick 2. \(X_1\)=no. electronic, \(X_2\)=no. memory.

\(x_1\backslash x_2\) 0 1 \(f_1(x_1)\)
0 1/10 2/10 3/10
1 4/10 2/10 6/10
2 1/10 0 1/10
\(f_2(x_2)\) 6/10 4/10 1
  • \(P(X_1+X_2\le1)=\frac{1+2+4}{10}=0.7\)
  • \(f_1(x_1\mid X_2=0)=\frac{f(x_1,0)}{6/10}\) → \(\frac16,\ \frac23,\ \frac16\)
  • \(f(2,1)=0\) but \(f_1(2)f_2(1)\ne0\) ⇒ not independent
Explanation

A joint distribution describes two random quantities at the same time, for example the numbers of electronic and memory defects in the same sample. It is usually shown as a table. Row totals and column totals give the marginal distributions, which describe each variable on its own and are found by adding out the other variable.

The conditional distribution restricts attention to one value of the other variable and rescales so that the probabilities add to 1. Independence means that knowing one variable tells you nothing about the other, so every cell equals the product of its row total and column total. To prove independence you must check all cells, but to disprove it one failing cell is enough. In the scanner example the cell \((2,1)\) is zero while the product of its marginals is not, so the variables are dependent.
