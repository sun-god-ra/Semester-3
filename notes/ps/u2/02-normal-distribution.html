Normal Distribution

Probability and Statistics · Unit II: Probability Densities and Joint Distributions

Normal Distribution
Subject
Probability and Statistics (24MA203)
Unit
Unit II: Probability Densities and Joint Distributions
Topic
2 of 7
Terms
Normal distribution, Standard deviation

Practice: Part B questions for Unit II and Part A short questions.

Notebook notes
  • Most important continuous dist. Bell shaped, symmetric about \(\mu\)

\[f(x)=\frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}\,e^{-\frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}},\quad -\infty<x<\infty\]

  • Written \(X\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2)\). Mean \(\mu\), S.D. \(\sigma\).
  • Properties (learn all):
    • curve symmetric about \(x=\mu\) → mean = median = mode
    • total area = 1; each half = 0.5
    • tails never touch the x-axis (asymptotic)
    • points of inflection at \(\mu\pm\sigma\)
    • bigger \(\sigma\) → flatter & wider curve
  • Empirical rule: 68% within \(\pm1\sigma\), 95% within \(\pm2\sigma\), 99.7% within \(\pm3\sigma\)
  • Standard normal \(Z\sim N(0,1)\) → convert using

\[Z=\frac{X-\mu}{\sigma}\]

  • Table gives \(\Phi(z)=P(Z\le z)\) (area to the left)
  • Table tricks:
    • \(P(Z>z)=1-\Phi(z)\)
    • \(\Phi(-z)=1-\Phi(z)\) (symmetry)
    • \(P(a<Z<b)=\Phi(b)-\Phi(a)\)

Normal curve: shaded parts show ±1σ (68%) and ±2σ (95%).

Worked example: \(\mu=16.28,\ \sigma=0.12\). Find \(P(16.00\le X\le16.50)\)

  • \(z_1=\frac{16.00-16.28}{0.12}=-2.33\), \(z_2=\frac{16.50-16.28}{0.12}=1.83\)
  • \(=\Phi(1.83)-\Phi(-2.33)=0.9664-0.0099=\mathbf{0.9565}\)

Reverse problem (find \(\mu\)): \(\sigma=10\), \(P(X<82.5)=0.8212\)

  • table: \(\Phi(z)=0.8212\Rightarrow z=0.92\)
  • \(0.92=\frac{82.5-\mu}{10}\Rightarrow\mu=73.3\)

Remember: "no. of students" = \(N\times P\) (e.g. \(300\times0.0912\approx27\))

Explanation

The normal distribution appears whenever a quantity is the sum of many small independent effects, such as measurement error, heights, exam marks or reaction times. It is completely described by two numbers: the centre \(\mu\) and the spread \(\sigma\). Changing \(\mu\) slides the curve left or right, and changing \(\sigma\) stretches or squeezes it without changing the total area.

There is no simple formula for the area under the curve, so tables are used for the standard normal distribution only. Standardising, \(Z=(X-\mu)/\sigma\), counts how many standard deviations a value is above or below the mean, and this lets one table serve every normal distribution. The table gives the area to the left of \(z\). For negative \(z\) you can use symmetry, \(\Phi(-z)=1-\Phi(z)\), if your table lists only positive values.

Questions come in three flavours. In the forward type you are given \(x\) and ask for a probability. In the reverse type you are given a probability and find \(x\) or an unknown parameter, so you read the table backwards. In the counting type you multiply the probability by the number of items, as in the 300-students example.
