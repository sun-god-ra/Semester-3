Poisson Process

Probability and Statistics · Unit I: Probability Distributions and Random Variables

Poisson Process
Subject
Probability and Statistics (24MA203)
Unit
Unit I: Probability Distributions and Random Variables
Topic
5 of 6
Terms
Poisson process

Practice: Part B questions for Unit I and Part A short questions.

Notebook notes
  • A Poisson process = events happening randomly over time (or space) at a constant average rate
  • Rate \(\alpha\) = average no. of events per unit time
  • Assumptions (3):
    • events in non-overlapping intervals are independent
    • prob. of exactly 1 event in a tiny interval \(\Delta t\) ≈ \(\alpha\,\Delta t\)
    • prob. of 2 or more in a tiny interval ≈ 0
  • No. of events in time \(T\) is Poisson with \(\lambda=\alpha T\)

\[P(X=x)=\frac{e^{-\alpha T}(\alpha T)^{x}}{x!}\]

  • Examples: calls at an exchange, defects per metre of cable, hits on a server, accidents per month
  • Time between events → exponential (extra info, not in syllabus)

Example: 6 requests/hour, find prob. of no request in 30 min

  • \(\lambda=\alpha T=6\times0.5=3\)
  • \(P(0)=e^{-3}=\mathbf{0.0498}\)
  • \(P(X\ge2)=1-e^{-3}(1+3)=\mathbf{0.8009}\)
Explanation

A Poisson process describes events that occur one at a time, at random moments, with a steady average rate. The three assumptions in the notes say that the past does not affect the future (independence), that events are rare in tiny intervals, and that two events never happen at exactly the same instant.

The key idea is that the count over a window of length \(T\) is Poisson with parameter \(\lambda=\alpha T\), so the rate must be scaled to the length of the window you are asking about. A common mistake is to use the hourly rate for a 30-minute question. In the example, 6 requests per hour over half an hour gives \(\lambda=3\), and then every question is a normal Poisson calculation.
