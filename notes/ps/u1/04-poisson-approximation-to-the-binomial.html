Poisson Approximation to the Binomial

Probability and Statistics · Unit I: Probability Distributions and Random Variables

Poisson Approximation to the Binomial
Subject
Probability and Statistics (24MA203)
Unit
Unit I: Probability Distributions and Random Variables
Topic
4 of 6
Terms
Variance, Poisson distribution

Practice: Part B questions for Unit I and Part A short questions.

Notebook notes
  • Poisson dist. counts rare events in a fixed interval (time / length / area)

\[p(x;\lambda)=\frac{e^{-\lambda}\lambda^{x}}{x!},\quad x=0,1,2,\dots\]

  • Mean = Variance = \(\lambda\) (imp. property)
  • When binomial has \(n\) large, \(p\) small → use Poisson with \(\lambda=np\)
    • thumb rule: \(n\ge 20\) and \(p\le 0.05\) (some books: \(n\ge100,\ np\le10\))
  • Why it works: \(n\to\infty,\ p\to0,\ np=\lambda\) fixed ⇒ \(b(x;n,p)\to p(x;\lambda)\)

Example: \(\lambda=1.8\)

  • \(P(X\ge1)=1-e^{-1.8}=\mathbf{0.8347}\)
  • \(P(X\le1)=e^{-1.8}(1+1.8)=\mathbf{0.4628}\)

Example (approx): \(n=200,\ p=0.01\) ⇒ \(\lambda=2\)

  • \(P(X=3)\approx\dfrac{e^{-2}2^3}{3!}=0.1804\) (exact binomial = 0.1814 → very close ✓)
Explanation

Computing \({}^{200}C_3(0.01)^3(0.99)^{197}\) by hand is painful. When \(n\) is large and \(p\) is small, the binomial probabilities almost exactly match the Poisson formula with \(\lambda=np\), which only needs \(e^{-\lambda}\) and a power of \(\lambda\). In the example with \(n=200\) and \(p=0.01\), the exact value is 0.1814 and the Poisson value is 0.1804.

The Poisson distribution has a special property that is asked in many exams: its mean and variance are both equal to \(\lambda\). If a question gives data where the sample mean and variance are almost equal, Poisson is a reasonable model. The distribution is used for counts of rare events in a fixed window, such as misprints per page or accidents per week.
