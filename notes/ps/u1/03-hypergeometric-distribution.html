Hypergeometric Distribution

Probability and Statistics · Unit I: Probability Distributions and Random Variables

Hypergeometric Distribution
Subject
Probability and Statistics (24MA203)
Unit
Unit I: Probability Distributions and Random Variables
Topic
3 of 6
Terms
Variance, Hypergeometric distribution

Practice: Part B questions for Unit I and Part A short questions.

Notebook notes
  • Use when we sample without replacement from a finite population
  • Trials are not independent (p changes after each draw) → so NOT binomial
  • Population \(N\) has \(K\) "successes" and \(N-K\) "failures". Draw \(n\) items.
  • \(X\) = no. of successes in the sample

\[h(x;n,N,K)=\frac{{}^{K}C_x\cdot{}^{N-K}C_{n-x}}{{}^{N}C_n}\]

  • Mean \(\mu=n\dfrac{K}{N}\)
  • Variance \(\sigma^2=\dfrac{N-n}{N-1}\cdot n\dfrac{K}{N}\Big(1-\dfrac{K}{N}\Big)\)
  • \(\dfrac{N-n}{N-1}\) = finite population correction
  • If \(N\) is very large compared to \(n\) → hypergeometric ≈ binomial with \(p=K/N\) (rule: \(n/N<0.05\))

Example: \(N=18,\ K=6,\ n=4\)

  • none defective: \(\dfrac{{}^6C_0\,{}^{12}C_4}{{}^{18}C_4}=\mathbf{0.1618}\)
  • exactly 1: \(\dfrac{{}^6C_1\,{}^{12}C_3}{{}^{18}C_4}=\mathbf{0.4314}\)
  • at least 3: \(P(3)+P(4)=\mathbf{0.0833}\)
Explanation

Imagine a box of 18 chips with 6 defective, and you pick 4 chips without putting any back. After each pick the proportion of defective chips left in the box changes, so the picks are not independent and the binomial formula does not apply. The hypergeometric distribution handles this exactly by counting: the number of ways to choose \(x\) defective chips from \(K\), times the number of ways to choose the remaining \(n-x\) good chips from \(N-K\), divided by the total number of ways to choose \(n\) chips from \(N\).

The mean is the same as the binomial mean with \(p=K/N\). The variance is smaller than the binomial one by the finite population correction \(\frac{N-n}{N-1}\), because sampling without replacement gives less variation. When the population is huge compared with the sample, the correction is almost 1 and the two distributions give nearly the same answers, which is why binomial is a good approximation in that case.
