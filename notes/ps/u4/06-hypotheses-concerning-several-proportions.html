Hypotheses Concerning Several Proportions

Probability and Statistics · Unit IV: Variances and Proportions

Hypotheses Concerning Several Proportions
Subject
Probability and Statistics (24MA203)
Unit
Unit IV: Variances and Proportions
Topic
6 of 9
Terms
Degrees of freedom

Practice: Part B questions for Unit IV and Part A short questions.

Notebook notes
  • Compare \(k\) population proportions: \(H_0:p_1=p_2=\dots=p_k\) ; \(H_1\): not all equal
  • Pooled estimate: \(\hat p=\dfrac{x_1+x_2+\dots+x_k}{n_1+n_2+\dots+n_k}\)
  • Expected counts: successes \(e_{1i}=n_i\hat p\), failures \(e_{2i}=n_i(1-\hat p)\)

\[\chi^2=\sum_{\text{all cells}}\frac{(f-e)^2}{e},\qquad \text{df}=k-1\]

  • Reject if \(\chi^2>\chi^2_\alpha\) (right tail only!)
  • Condition: every \(e\ge5\)
  • For \(k=2\) this is the same as the two-proportion \(z\)-test (\(\chi^2=z^2\))

Example: successes out of 200 in 3 groups: 52, 64, 84

  • \(\hat p=\frac{200}{600}=0.3333\), expected successes \(=66.67\) each, failures \(=133.33\) each
  • \(\chi^2=3.23+0.11+4.51+1.61+0.05+2.25=11.76\), df = 2, \(\chi^2_{0.05}=5.991\)
  • reject → proportions are not all equal
Explanation

With three or more groups, comparing them two at a time would create many tests and raise the chance of a false alarm. The chi-square test handles all groups at once. Under \(H_0\) all groups share one common success rate, estimated by pooling the data. We then compare the counts we observed with the counts we would expect from that common rate. Large differences give a large chi-square value.

Only large values are evidence against \(H_0\), so the test is always right-tailed. The degrees of freedom are \(k-1\) because once \(k-1\) of the group rates are fixed, the last one is determined by the pooled rate.
