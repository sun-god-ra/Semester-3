Hypotheses Concerning One Proportion

Probability and Statistics · Unit IV: Variances and Proportions

Hypotheses Concerning One Proportion
Subject
Probability and Statistics (24MA203)
Unit
Unit IV: Variances and Proportions
Topic
5 of 9
Terms
Variance, Standard deviation, Confidence interval

Practice: Part B questions for Unit IV and Part A short questions.

Notebook notes
  • \(H_0:p=p_0\) ; large \(n\) (\(np_0\ge5\), \(n(1-p_0)\ge5\))

\[z=\frac{x-np_0}{\sqrt{np_0(1-p_0)}}\ =\ \frac{\hat p-p_0}{\sqrt{p_0(1-p_0)/n}}\]

  • Use \(p_0\) (not \(\hat p\)) in the denominator for a test; use \(\hat p\) for a CI
  • Rejection region same as z-test for mean
  • Small \(n\) → use exact binomial probabilities

Example: \(H_0:p=0.5\), \(H_1:p\ne0.5\), \(x=62\) of \(n=100\), \(\alpha=0.05\)

  • \(z=\frac{62-50}{\sqrt{100(0.5)(0.5)}}=\frac{12}{5}=2.4\)
  • \(2.4>1.96\) → reject \(H_0\)
Explanation

The test asks whether the observed fraction is far enough from the claimed fraction to be unlikely by chance. If \(H_0\) is true, the number of successes is binomial with mean \(np_0\) and variance \(np_0(1-p_0)\), so the normal approximation gives the \(z\) statistic directly. Notice that the claimed value \(p_0\) is used for the standard deviation, because we compute everything as if \(H_0\) were true. In a confidence interval there is no claim, so \(\hat p\) is used instead.
