Estimation of Variances

Probability and Statistics · Unit IV: Variances and Proportions

Estimation of Variances
Subject
Probability and Statistics (24MA203)
Unit
Unit IV: Variances and Proportions
Topic
1 of 9
Terms
Variance, Chi-square distribution, Degrees of freedom, Confidence interval

Practice: Part B questions for Unit IV and Part A short questions.

Notebook notes
  • Used when we care about spread / consistency (e.g. variation in machine output), not the mean.
  • Point estimate of \(\sigma^2\) = sample variance \(s^2=\dfrac{\sum(x_i-\bar x)^2}{n-1}\)
  • Chi-square distribution \(\chi^2_\nu\): skewed right, only positive values, shape depends on df \(\nu\)
  • If population is normal:

\[\chi^2=\frac{(n-1)s^2}{\sigma^2}\ \sim\ \chi^2_{\,n-1}\]

  • CI for \(\sigma^2\):

\[\frac{(n-1)s^2}{\chi^2_{\alpha/2}}\ <\ \sigma^2\ <\ \frac{(n-1)s^2}{\chi^2_{1-\alpha/2}}\]

  • CI for \(\sigma\) → take square roots of both ends.
  • Note: bigger \(\chi^2\) value goes in the lower limit (denominator). Easy to mix up!
  • Not symmetric about \(s^2\) (chi-square is skewed)
  • \(\chi^2_\alpha\) = value with area \(\alpha\) to its right
df \(\chi^2_{0.05}\) \(\chi^2_{0.95}\) \(\chi^2_{0.025}\) \(\chi^2_{0.975}\)
5 11.070 1.145 12.833 0.831
9 16.919 3.325 19.023 2.700
10 18.307 3.940 20.483 3.247
15 24.996 7.261 27.488 6.262
20 31.410 10.851 34.170 9.591

Example: \(n=10,\ s^2=4.2\), 95% CI for \(\sigma^2\) (df = 9)

  • lower \(=\frac{9(4.2)}{19.023}=1.99\), upper \(=\frac{9(4.2)}{2.700}=14.0\)
  • \(1.99<\sigma^2<14.0\)
Explanation

Means tell us about the centre of a population, but quality control and risk questions are often about the spread. A machine that fills bottles with the right average amount but with a wide spread still produces bottles that are too full or too empty. The sample variance estimates the population variance, but its sampling behaviour is not normal. Under a normal population, the scaled quantity \((n-1)s^2/\sigma^2\) follows a chi-square distribution with \(n-1\) degrees of freedom.

The chi-square curve is lopsided, with a long tail on the right, so the confidence interval for \(\sigma^2\) is not centred on \(s^2\). To build it, you divide \((n-1)s^2\) by two chi-square values taken from the table. The larger table value gives the lower end of the interval and the smaller one gives the upper end. A common slip is to swap them. A quick sanity check is that \(s^2\) must lie between the two limits.
