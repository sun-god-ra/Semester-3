Estimation of Proportions

Probability and Statistics · Unit IV: Variances and Proportions

Estimation of Proportions
Subject
Probability and Statistics (24MA203)
Unit
Unit IV: Variances and Proportions
Topic
4 of 9

Practice: Part B questions for Unit IV and Part A short questions.

Notebook notes
  • Proportion \(p\) = fraction of population with a property. Sample: \(\hat p=\dfrac{x}{n}\) (unbiased for \(p\))
  • For large \(n\): \(\hat p\) ≈ normal with mean \(p\) and sd \(\sqrt{\dfrac{p(1-p)}{n}}\) (binomial → normal)
  • Large-sample CI:

\[\hat p\ \pm\ z_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{\frac{\hat p(1-\hat p)}{n}}\]

  • Max error \(E=z_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{\dfrac{\hat p(1-\hat p)}{n}}\)
  • Sample size
    • if a rough \(p\) is known: \(n=p(1-p)\left(\dfrac{z_{\alpha/2}}{E}\right)^2\)
    • if no idea → worst case \(p=\tfrac12\) : \(n=\dfrac14\left(\dfrac{z_{\alpha/2}}{E}\right)^2\)
  • Round up. Condition: \(n\hat p\ge5\) and \(n(1-\hat p)\ge5\)

Example: 45 defectives in 200, 95% CI

  • \(\hat p=0.225\), \(SE=\sqrt{\frac{0.225\times0.775}{200}}=0.0295\)
  • CI \(=0.225\pm0.058=(0.167,\ 0.283)\)
  • sample size for \(E=0.03\), no prior: \(\frac14\left(\frac{1.96}{0.03}\right)^2=1067.1\Rightarrow n=1068\)
Explanation

A proportion is an average of 0/1 data, which is why its estimate behaves like a sample mean and becomes approximately normal for large samples. Its standard error is \(\sqrt{p(1-p)/n}\), which depends on the unknown \(p\), so for the interval we replace \(p\) with \(\hat p\).

The sample-size formula shows how survey sizes are chosen. The product \(p(1-p)\) is largest when \(p=0.5\), so using 0.5 gives a sample size that is safe for any true value of \(p\). This is why opinion polls with a 3% margin of error and 95% confidence need roughly 1,000 respondents, whatever the population.
