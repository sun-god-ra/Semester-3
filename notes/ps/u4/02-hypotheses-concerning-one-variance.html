Hypotheses Concerning One Variance

Probability and Statistics · Unit IV: Variances and Proportions

Hypotheses Concerning One Variance
Subject
Probability and Statistics (24MA203)
Unit
Unit IV: Variances and Proportions
Topic
2 of 9
Terms
Standard deviation, Variance, Degrees of freedom

Practice: Part B questions for Unit IV and Part A short questions.

Notebook notes
  • \(H_0:\sigma^2=\sigma_0^2\)

\[\chi^2=\frac{(n-1)s^2}{\sigma_0^2},\qquad \text{df}=n-1\]

\(H_1\) Reject \(H_0\) if
\(\sigma^2>\sigma_0^2\) \(\chi^2>\chi^2_\alpha\)
\(\sigma^2<\sigma_0^2\) \(\chi^2<\chi^2_{1-\alpha}\)
\(\sigma^2\ne\sigma_0^2\) \(\chi^2>\chi^2_{\alpha/2}\) or \(\chi^2<\chi^2_{1-\alpha/2}\)
  • Assumes normal population (not robust if it isn't!)

Example: \(H_0:\sigma^2=9\), \(H_1:\sigma^2>9\), \(n=16\), \(s^2=14.5\), \(\alpha=0.05\)

  • \(\chi^2=\frac{15(14.5)}{9}=24.17\); \(\chi^2_{0.05}(15)=24.996\)
  • \(24.17<24.996\) → fail to reject (just short!)
Explanation

This test checks a claim such as "the standard deviation of the process is at most 3 mm". The statistic compares the sample variance with the claimed variance, scaled by the degrees of freedom. A value close to \(n-1\) means the sample variance matches the claim. A much larger value suggests that the true variance is bigger, and a much smaller one suggests it is smaller.

In the example the statistic is 24.17 and the critical value is 24.996, so the result is very close to the boundary but does not reach it. The correct wording is that there is not enough evidence at the 5% level to say that the variance exceeds 9. The test relies strongly on the population being normal, so it should not be trusted on clearly skewed data.
