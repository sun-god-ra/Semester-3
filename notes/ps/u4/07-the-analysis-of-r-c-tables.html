The Analysis of r × c Tables

Probability and Statistics · Unit IV: Variances and Proportions

The Analysis of r × c Tables
Subject
Probability and Statistics (24MA203)
Unit
Unit IV: Variances and Proportions
Topic
7 of 9
Terms
Contingency table, Degrees of freedom

Practice: Part B questions for Unit IV and Part A short questions.

Notebook notes
  • \(r\) rows, \(c\) columns of counts (contingency table)
  • Two uses:
    • Test of independence: one sample, two attributes. \(H_0\): attributes independent
    • Test of homogeneity: several samples, same categories. \(H_0\): same distribution
  • Same calculation both ways:

\[e_{ij}=\frac{(\text{row } i\text{ total})(\text{col } j\text{ total})}{\text{grand total}}\]

\[\chi^2=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{c}\frac{(f_{ij}-e_{ij})^2}{e_{ij}},\qquad \text{df}=(r-1)(c-1)\]

  • Right-tailed test. Need every \(e_{ij}\ge5\) (else combine categories)

Example: gender vs choice of product

A B C total
Male 30 20 10 60
Female 20 40 30 90
total 50 60 40 150
  • expected (Male): \(\frac{60\times50}{150}=20,\ 24,\ 16\) ; (Female): \(30,\ 36,\ 24\)
  • \(\chi^2=5+0.67+2.25+3.33+0.44+1.5=13.19\), df \(=(2-1)(3-1)=2\)
  • \(13.19>5.991\) → choice depends on gender (reject independence)
Explanation

A contingency table counts people in two classifications at once, for example gender and product choice. If the two classifications were independent, then the expected count in each cell would be the row share times the column share times the grand total, which simplifies to the formula in the notes. The statistic adds up the squared gaps between what was observed and what was expected, each scaled by the expected count.

The degrees of freedom \((r-1)(c-1)\) come from the fact that row and column totals are fixed, so only that many cells can vary freely. In the example, the value 13.19 is well above 5.991, so the choice of product is related to gender. The test says that a relation exists but not how strong it is. To see the pattern, compare the cells with the biggest contributions to the sum, here the Male–C and Female–A cells.
