Hypotheses Concerning Two Variances

Probability and Statistics · Unit IV: Variances and Proportions

Hypotheses Concerning Two Variances
Subject
Probability and Statistics (24MA203)
Unit
Unit IV: Variances and Proportions
Topic
3 of 9
Terms
F-distribution, Variance, Swapping, Degrees of freedom

Practice: Part B questions for Unit IV and Part A short questions.

Notebook notes
  • Compare spread of two normal populations. \(H_0:\sigma_1^2=\sigma_2^2\)
  • F-distribution: ratio of two chi-squares, right skewed, df pair \((\nu_1,\nu_2)\) — order matters!

\[F=\frac{s_1^2}{s_2^2},\qquad \nu_1=n_1-1,\ \nu_2=n_2-1\]

  • Trick: put the larger sample variance on top → then only the right tail needed, \(F\ge1\)
  • Reciprocal property: \(F_{1-\alpha}(\nu_1,\nu_2)=\dfrac{1}{F_{\alpha}(\nu_2,\nu_1)}\) (swap df!)
df \((\nu_1,\nu_2)\) \(F_{0.05}\) \(F_{0.025}\)
(9, 9) 3.18 4.03
(8, 10) 3.07 3.85
(10, 10) 2.98 3.72
(5, 10) 3.33 4.24
  • Two-tailed test at level \(\alpha\) with larger variance on top: reject if \(F>F_{\alpha/2}(\nu_1,\nu_2)\)
  • CI for \(\dfrac{\sigma_1^2}{\sigma_2^2}\): \(\ \dfrac{s_1^2}{s_2^2}\cdot\dfrac{1}{F_{\alpha/2}(\nu_1,\nu_2)}\ <\ \dfrac{\sigma_1^2}{\sigma_2^2}\ <\ \dfrac{s_1^2}{s_2^2}\,F_{\alpha/2}(\nu_2,\nu_1)\)

Example: \(s_1^2=8.4\ (n_1=10),\ s_2^2=3.5\ (n_2=10)\), \(H_1:\sigma_1^2>\sigma_2^2\), \(\alpha=0.05\)

  • \(F=\frac{8.4}{3.5}=2.40\), df \((9,9)\), \(F_{0.05}=3.18\)
  • \(2.40<3.18\) → fail to reject
Explanation

To compare the spread of two populations you take the ratio of their sample variances. If the true variances are equal, the ratio is close to 1, and its distribution is the F distribution. This distribution has two degrees-of-freedom numbers, one for the numerator and one for the denominator, and swapping them gives a different curve. Tables list only upper-tail values, so the usual trick is to place the larger sample variance in the numerator, which makes \(F\ge1\) and lets you use the upper tail.

When you need a lower-tail value, the reciprocal rule converts it into an upper-tail value with the degrees of freedom swapped. For a two-tailed test at level \(\alpha\), split the level into two halves and use \(F_{\alpha/2}\) with the larger variance on top.
