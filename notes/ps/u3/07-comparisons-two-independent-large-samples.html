Comparisons: Two Independent Large Samples

Probability and Statistics · Unit III: Statistical Inference

Comparisons: Two Independent Large Samples
Subject
Probability and Statistics (24MA203)
Unit
Unit III: Statistical Inference
Topic
7 of 11
Terms
Variance, Null hypothesis

Practice: Part B questions for Unit III and Part A short questions.

Notebook notes
  • Compare \(\mu_1\) and \(\mu_2\) from two independent samples, both \(n_1,n_2\ge30\)
  • \(H_0:\mu_1-\mu_2=\delta_0\) (usually \(\delta_0=0\))

\[z=\frac{(\bar x_1-\bar x_2)-\delta_0}{\sqrt{\dfrac{\sigma_1^2}{n_1}+\dfrac{\sigma_2^2}{n_2}}}\]

  • replace \(\sigma_i^2\) by \(s_i^2\) if unknown
  • CI: \((\bar x_1-\bar x_2)\pm z_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{\dfrac{s_1^2}{n_1}+\dfrac{s_2^2}{n_2}}\)
  • Example: \(n_1=40,\bar x_1=82,s_1=8\); \(n_2=50,\bar x_2=78,s_2=10\)
    • SE \(=\sqrt{1.6+2.0}=1.897\), \(z=\frac{4}{1.897}=2.11\) → reject at 0.05 (two-tailed, 1.96)
Explanation

To compare two populations you look at the difference of the sample means. Since the samples are independent, the variance of the difference is the sum of the two variances of the means, so the standard error is \(\sqrt{s_1^2/n_1+s_2^2/n_2}\). Large samples let you use \(z\) and replace the unknown variances by sample variances. If the null hypothesis is "no difference", then \(\delta_0=0\) and the statistic is simply the observed difference divided by its standard error.
