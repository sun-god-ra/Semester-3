Maximum Error and Sample Size

Probability and Statistics · Unit III: Statistical Inference

Maximum Error and Sample Size
Subject
Probability and Statistics (24MA203)
Unit
Unit III: Statistical Inference
Topic
10 of 11
Terms
Confidence interval

Practice: Part B questions for Unit III and Part A short questions.

Notebook notes
  • With probability \(1-\alpha\): \(|\bar X-\mu|\le E\) where
    • \(E=z_{\alpha/2}\dfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt n}\) (\(\sigma\) known)
    • \(E=t_{\alpha/2}\dfrac{s}{\sqrt n}\) (\(\sigma\) unknown, \(n-1\) d.f.)
  • Sample size to keep the error within \(E\): \(n=\left(\dfrac{z_{\alpha/2}\,\sigma}{E}\right)^2\). Always round up (a bigger sample only helps).

Examples

  • \(n=150,\ \sigma=6.2\), 99%: \(E=2.575\times\frac{6.2}{\sqrt{150}}=\mathbf{1.30}\)
  • 95% confidence, \(E=0.5\) min, \(\sigma=1.6\) min: \(n=\left(\frac{1.96\times1.6}{0.5}\right)^2=39.34\to\mathbf{40}\)

⚠ Slide wording slip: "at most 40 mechanics". It should be "at least 40", because 39.34 must be rounded up.

  • Tin melting point: \(n=6,\ \bar x=230.26,\ s=0.14\), 98%: \(t_{0.01}(5)=3.365\), \(E=3.365\times\frac{0.14}{\sqrt6}=\mathbf{0.19}^\circ\)C
  • Lead contamination readings: 2.4, 2.9, 2.7, 2.6, 2.9, 2.0, 2.8, 2.2, 2.4, 2.4, 2.0, 2.5. Sum 29.8, \(\bar x=\mathbf{2.483}\), \(s=0.559\), estimated standard error \(=\frac{0.559}{\sqrt{12}}=\mathbf{0.161}\).

The true spiked concentration is 1.25, so the readings average well above it (2.48). That gap is what the experiment was meant to reveal: some lead is coming from the background or the method.

Explanation

The maximum error is just half the width of the confidence interval. The sample-size formula comes from solving \(E=z\sigma/\sqrt n\) for \(n\). Because \(n\) sits under a square root, halving the error needs four times the sample. And since you can never take 39.34 observations, round up, not to the nearest whole number.
