Experiment 10 — Ring counter and Johnson counter

Digital Logic Design Lab · Lab: Tasks 1 to 12

Experiment 10 — Ring counter and Johnson counter
Subject
Digital Logic Design Lab (24CS282)
Unit
Lab: Tasks 1 to 12
Topic
10 of 12
Terms
Ring counter, Flip-flop, Shift register, Decoder, Johnson counter, Verilog
Notebook notes
  • Aim: Build and verify a 4-bit ring counter and a 4-bit Johnson (twisted-ring) counter.
  • Components: IC 7474 (dual D flip-flop) ×2 or 74194 universal shift register; clock pulser; LEDs
  • Theory
    • Ring counter: a shift register whose last output is fed back to the first input (\(D_0=Q_{n-1}\)). It must be preset to a single 1 (1000). The 1 circulates, so \(n\) flip-flops give only \(n\) states (MOD-n). Outputs are already decoded (one-hot) — no extra decoder needed
    • Johnson counter (twisted ring / switch-tail): feedback from the complement of the last output (\(D_0=\overline{Q_{n-1}}\)). Starting from 0000 it has \(2n\) states (MOD-2n): 0000, 1000, 1100, 1110, 1111, 0111, 0011, 0001. Each state can be decoded by a 2-input AND gate, and only one bit changes per clock (glitch-free decoding)
    • Self-starting: the ring counter and the Johnson counter must be initialised (preset or clear), otherwise they may start in an unused state and stay in an invalid loop (the 4-bit Johnson counter has 16 − 8 = 8 unused states that form separate loops), or they need extra gating to self-correct
    • Frequency of each output = clock/n (ring) or clock/2n (Johnson), with a duty cycle of 1/n (ring) or 50 % (Johnson)
  • Procedure
    • Ring: connect Q of each FF to D of the next, Q of the last to D of the first; use Preset on the first FF and Clear on the others to load 1000; give pulses
    • Johnson: connect \(\overline{Q}\) of the last FF to D of the first; Clear all FFs; give pulses and note the 8 states
verilog
// Task 10: ring counter and Johnson (twisted-ring) counter, 4 bit
module dff(input clk, set, rst, d, output reg q);
  always @(posedge clk or posedge set or posedge rst)
    if (rst) q <= 1'b0;
    else if (set) q <= 1'b1;
    else q <= d;
endmodule

module ring4(input clk, init, output [3:0] q);          // init presets 1000
  dff f3(clk, init, 1'b0, q[0], q[3]);
  dff f2(clk, 1'b0, init, q[3], q[2]);
  dff f1(clk, 1'b0, init, q[2], q[1]);
  dff f0(clk, 1'b0, init, q[1], q[0]);
endmodule

module johnson4(input clk, init, output [3:0] q);       // init clears to 0000, feedback from Q0'
  dff f3(clk, 1'b0, init, ~q[0], q[3]);
  dff f2(clk, 1'b0, init, q[3], q[2]);
  dff f1(clk, 1'b0, init, q[2], q[1]);
  dff f0(clk, 1'b0, init, q[1], q[0]);
endmodule

  1   | 0100   1000

  4   | 1000   1111
  5   | 0100   0111

  9   | 0100   1000
  • Precautions: switch off the supply before changing wiring; check Vcc/GND; tie unused inputs; confirm LED polarity; no loose wires
  • Result: the observed outputs matched the truth table, so the circuit works as the theory predicts
  • Viva questions
    • How many states in a 4-bit ring and Johnson counter? (4 and 8)
    • What is the advantage of the Johnson counter over the ring counter? (more states per FF, simple decoding)
    • Why must the ring counter be preset?
    • Which counter is used as a one-hot sequence generator?
    • How many unused states in a 4-bit Johnson counter? (8)
Explanation

The 1000 pattern of the ring counter moves one place at every clock pulse and returns to the beginning after four pulses. In the Johnson counter the inverted last output is fed back, so the register fills with 1s one bit at a time and then empties with 0s, giving eight states for four flip-flops. In the table, the ring counter shows 1000, 0100, 0010, 0001, 1000 (period 4), and the Johnson counter shows 0000, 1000, 1100, 1110, 1111, 0111, 0011, 0001, 0000 (period 8).
