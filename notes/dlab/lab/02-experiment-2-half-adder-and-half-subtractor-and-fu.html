Experiment 2 — Half adder and half subtractor (and full adder / full subtractor)

Digital Logic Design Lab · Lab: Tasks 1 to 12

Experiment 2 — Half adder and half subtractor (and full adder / full subtractor)
Subject
Digital Logic Design Lab (24CS282)
Unit
Lab: Tasks 1 to 12
Topic
2 of 12
Terms
Half adder, Full adder, XOR, Verilog, Minterm
Notebook notes
  • Aim: Design, wire and verify the half adder and half subtractor, and extend them to the full adder and full subtractor.
  • Components: IC 7486 (XOR), 7408 (AND), 7404 (NOT), 7432 (OR); trainer kit
  • Theory
    • Half adder: \(S=A\oplus B\), \(C=AB\). Half subtractor: \(D=A\oplus B\), \(B_{out}=\overline{A}B\)
    • Full adder: \(S=A\oplus B\oplus C_{in}\), \(C_{out}=AB+C_{in}(A\oplus B)\) — built from two half adders and an OR. Full subtractor: \(D=A\oplus B\oplus B_{in}\), \(B_{out}=\overline{A}B+\overline{(A\oplus B)}B_{in}\) — two half subtractors and an OR
    • A half adder cannot add a carry-in; that is why the full adder is needed for multi-bit addition. Half subtractor has no borrow-in
  • Procedure
    • Wire the XOR and AND gates as half adder; the XOR and (NOT + AND) as half subtractor; apply the 4 combinations and note outputs
    • Join two half adders and an OR gate for the full adder; apply the 8 combinations of A, B, Cin
    • Likewise for the full subtractor
    • Compare with the truth tables
verilog
// Task 2: half adder, half subtractor, full adder, full subtractor
module half_adder(input a, b, output sum, carry);
  xor (sum, a, b);
  and (carry, a, b);
endmodule

module half_subtractor(input a, b, output diff, borrow);
  wire na;
  xor (diff, a, b);
  not (na, a);
  and (borrow, na, b);
endmodule

module full_adder(input a, b, cin, output sum, cout);
  wire s1, c1, c2;
  half_adder ha1(a, b, s1, c1);
  half_adder ha2(s1, cin, sum, c2);
  or (cout, c1, c2);
endmodule

module full_subtractor(input a, b, bin, output diff, bout);
  wire d1, b1, b2;
  half_subtractor hs1(a, b, d1, b1);
  half_subtractor hs2(d1, bin, diff, b2);
  or (bout, b1, b2);
endmodule

  • Precautions: switch off the supply before changing wiring; check Vcc/GND; tie unused inputs; confirm LED polarity; no loose wires
  • Result: the observed outputs matched the truth table, so the circuit works as the theory predicts
  • Viva questions
    • Why is it called a half adder?
    • Draw the full adder using two half adders
    • How many 7486 and 7408 ICs do you need for a full adder?
    • Express \(C_{out}\) in the sum of minterms (3, 5, 6, 7)
    • Write \(B_{out}\) of the full subtractor as a sum of minterms (1, 2, 3, 7)
Explanation

The half adder adds two bits and gives a sum (XOR) and a carry (AND). 1+1 gives sum 0 and carry 1. The half subtractor computes A−B. The difference is again XOR, but the borrow is \(\overline{A}B\), and it is 1 only for 0−1. The full adder can be seen as two half adders in series: the first adds A and B, the second adds its sum and the carry-in, and the OR gate combines the two carries (they cannot both be 1). The output table shows that for inputs 1,1,1 the sum is 1 and the carry is 1 (1+1+1 = 3 = 11 in binary). The full subtractor shows difference 1 and borrow 1 for 1,1,1 (1−1−1 = −1, so we borrow 1 and the difference is 1).
