Experiment 8 — Synchronous counters: up, down, up/down and MOD-N

Digital Logic Design Lab · Lab: Tasks 1 to 12

Experiment 8 — Synchronous counters: up, down, up/down and MOD-N
Subject
Digital Logic Design Lab (24CS282)
Unit
Lab: Tasks 1 to 12
Topic
8 of 12
Terms
Jk flip-flop, BCD, Flip-flop, K-map, Verilog, Propagation delay
Notebook notes
  • Aim: Design and verify synchronous 3-bit up, down and up/down counters and a MOD-6 counter with JK flip-flops.
  • Components: IC 7476 / 7473 (JK) ×2, 7408 (AND), 7404, 7486 / 7432 for the up/down control; clock pulser, LEDs. (Ready-made: 74193 up/down binary, 74192 BCD)
  • Theory
    • In a synchronous counter all flip-flops share the same clock — no ripple delay, so higher maximum frequency than asynchronous counters; extra gates are needed to decide when each flip-flop toggles
    • Up counter (3-bit): \(J_0=K_0=1\); \(J_1=K_1=Q_0\); \(J_2=K_2=Q_1Q_0\). Down counter: \(J_1=K_1=\overline{Q_0}\); \(J_2=K_2=\overline{Q_1}\,\overline{Q_0}\). Up/down with control M (M=1 up, M=0 down): \(J_1=K_1=MQ_0+\overline{M}\,\overline{Q_0}\), \(J_2=K_2=MQ_1Q_0+\overline{M}\,\overline{Q_1}\,\overline{Q_0}\)
    • MOD-6 (0 to 5) by the design procedure (state table, JK excitation table, K-maps with don't-care for 110, 111): \(J_2=Q_1Q_0\), \(K_2=Q_0\); \(J_1=\overline{Q_2}Q_0\), \(K_1=Q_0\); \(J_0=K_0=1\). It self-starts: from 110 → 111 → 000, from the unused states it returns to the valid cycle (check!)
    • Counter facts: an \(n\)-bit counter has modulus \(2^n\); MOD-N needs \(\lceil\log_2 N\rceil\) flip-flops; each flip-flop divides the clock frequency by 2
  • Procedure
    • Connect the clock to all CLK pins; set all flip-flops to 0 using Clear
    • Wire the AND gates as per the equations; give pulses one by one; note the LED states
    • For up/down wire the control switch M with the extra gates; verify counting in both directions
    • For MOD-6 wire the J, K equations and verify the 0→5→0 cycle
verilog
// Task 8: synchronous counters (JK flip-flops)
module jkff(input clk, rst, j, k, output reg q);
  always @(posedge clk or posedge rst)
    if (rst) q <= 1'b0;
    else case ({j, k})
      2'b00: q <= q;
      2'b01: q <= 1'b0;
      2'b10: q <= 1'b1;
      2'b11: q <= ~q;
    endcase
endmodule

// 3-bit synchronous UP counter: J0=K0=1, J1=K1=Q0, J2=K2=Q1.Q0
module sync_up(input clk, rst, output [2:0] q);
  jkff f0(clk, rst, 1'b1, 1'b1, q[0]);
  jkff f1(clk, rst, q[0], q[0], q[1]);
  jkff f2(clk, rst, q[1] & q[0], q[1] & q[0], q[2]);
endmodule

// 3-bit synchronous DOWN counter: J1=K1=Q0', J2=K2=Q1'.Q0'
module sync_down(input clk, rst, output [2:0] q);
  jkff f0(clk, rst, 1'b1, 1'b1, q[0]);
  jkff f1(clk, rst, ~q[0], ~q[0], q[1]);
  jkff f2(clk, rst, ~q[1] & ~q[0], ~q[1] & ~q[0], q[2]);
endmodule

// 3-bit UP/DOWN counter: M=1 up, M=0 down
module sync_updown(input clk, rst, m, output [2:0] q);
  wire t1 = m ? q[0] : ~q[0];
  wire t2 = m ? (q[1] & q[0]) : (~q[1] & ~q[0]);
  jkff f0(clk, rst, 1'b1, 1'b1, q[0]);
  jkff f1(clk, rst, t1, t1, q[1]);
  jkff f2(clk, rst, t2, t2, q[2]);
endmodule

// MOD-6 synchronous counter (0..5): J2 = Q1.Q0, K2 = Q0, J1 = Q2'.Q0, K1 = Q0, J0 = K0 = 1
module sync_mod6(input clk, rst, output [2:0] q);
  jkff f0(clk, rst, 1'b1, 1'b1, q[0]);
  jkff f1(clk, rst, ~q[2] & q[0], q[0], q[1]);
  jkff f2(clk, rst, q[1] & q[0], q[0], q[2]);
endmodule

  • Precautions: switch off the supply before changing wiring; check Vcc/GND; tie unused inputs; confirm LED polarity; no loose wires
  • Result: the observed outputs matched the truth table, so the circuit works as the theory predicts
  • Viva questions
    • Why is a synchronous counter faster than an asynchronous one? (no cumulative propagation delay)
    • Design a MOD-5 synchronous counter (3 FFs)
    • What is lock-out and self-starting?
    • Maximum count of an \(n\)-bit counter? (\(2^n-1\))
    • What is the 'excitation table' of a JK flip-flop? (0→0: J=0,K=X; 0→1: J=1,K=X; 1→0: J=X,K=1; 1→1: J=X,K=0)
Explanation

In a synchronous counter the same clock reaches every flip-flop, and a flip-flop is told to toggle (J=K=1) only when all the lower bits are 1. Therefore bit 1 toggles when Q0 is 1 and bit 2 toggles when Q1 and Q0 are both 1. The table shows the up counter counting 000 to 111 and wrapping, while the down counter starts from 000 and the first pulse takes it to 111. In the up/down counter the control M selects the toggling conditions for the counting direction. The MOD-6 counter counts 000 to 101 and then returns to 000 on the sixth pulse: the equations were derived from the excitation table, with the unused states 110 and 111 as don't-care. Look at the last lines of the table: it goes 5 (101) → 0 (000).
