Lab

Tasks 1 to 12

Digital Logic Design Lab · 24CS282

Syllabus: Twelve tasks: gates, adders, code converters, comparators, decoders, MUX, flip-flops, counters, registers, FPGA.

Topics in this unit

  1. Experiment 1 — Logic gates using ICs; NAND and NOR as universal gatesAim: Verify the truth tables of the basic gates AND, OR, NOT, NAND, NOR, XOR, XNOR with ICs, and realise NOT, AND and OR using only NAND gates and onl…
  2. Experiment 2 — Half adder and half subtractor (and full adder / full subtractor)Aim: Design, wire and verify the half adder and half subtractor, and extend them to the full adder and full subtractor.
  3. Experiment 3 — Code converters: Binary ↔ Gray, BCD ↔ Excess-3Aim: Design and implement a 4-bit binary-to-Gray converter and a Gray-to-binary converter, and BCD-to-Excess-3 and Excess-3-to-BCD converters.
  4. Experiment 4 — Single-bit comparatorAim: Design and verify a 1-bit magnitude comparator with outputs A>B, A=B and A<B (and extend to a 4-bit comparator with IC 7485).
  5. Experiment 5 — Decoders and encodersAim: Implement decoders (2×4, 3×8, BCD-to-decimal, BCD-to-7-segment) and encoders (4×2, octal-to-binary, decimal-to-BCD).
  6. Experiment 6 — Multiplexers and demultiplexersAim: Implement 2×1 and 4×1 multiplexers, and 1×2 and 1×4 demultiplexers.
  7. Experiment 7 — Latches and flip-flops: SR latch, SR, JK, D, T and master-slave JKAim: Study the SR latch (NAND and NOR), the clocked SR, JK, D and T flip-flops, and the master-slave JK flip-flop.
  8. Experiment 8 — Synchronous counters: up, down, up/down and MOD-NAim: Design and verify synchronous 3-bit up, down and up/down counters and a MOD-6 counter with JK flip-flops.
  9. Experiment 9 — Asynchronous (ripple) counters: up, down and MOD-NAim: Design and verify 3-bit asynchronous up and down counters and a MOD-6 counter.
  10. Experiment 10 — Ring counter and Johnson counterAim: Build and verify a 4-bit ring counter and a 4-bit Johnson (twisted-ring) counter.
  11. Experiment 11 — Shift registers: SISO, SIPO, PISO and PIPOAim: Verify the four modes of a 4-bit shift register using D flip-flops (IC 7474 / 7495 / 74194 / 74164 / 74165).

Common Notes: how to read a lab experiment

All these experiments only use the theory of Units I to V of the DLD course. The theory behind each is explained in the DLD notes. The most common mistakes in the lab are: a missing common ground between the trainer kit and the ICs, wrong supply pins, using an output as an input, using an unused input floating, and a missing clock edge or the wrong edge. In viva, be ready to state the IC number, the pin numbers of Vcc and GND, the truth table, and the Boolean expression for the circuit that you wired.
