Understanding Nature and Existence

Universal Human Values II · Unit IV: Harmony in Nature and Existence

Understanding Nature and Existence
Subject
Universal Human Values II (24UC201)
Unit
Unit IV: Harmony in Nature and Existence
Topic
1 of 7

Practice: Part B questions for Unit IV and Part A short questions.

Notebook notes
  • Existence = everything that is (all units: material, plants, animals, humans) in the all-pervasive space. Nature = the collection of all units other than the human being's 'I'; in the course, Nature = the four orders (material, bio, animal, human), all of them interconnected
  • Unit = any entity (a stone, a plant, an animal, a human, the earth). Each unit has form, properties, natural characteristics (svabhav) and activities
  • Two aspects of every unit: Body (material, made of the 4 elements) and, in the higher orders, the 'I' (sentient) — see Unit II
Explanation

Think of it as a ladder: Material → Bio → Animal → Human. A stone only exists and interacts (composition–decomposition). A plant also grows and makes seeds. An animal also has desires and moves around with instincts. A human also has understanding and choice. The order is not a ranking of importance; each order is needed by the others (we need plants and soil). For the exam: table with examples and activities of each order.

A key distinction: animals behave per their nature so they are in harmony automatically (no one needs to teach a cow to be a cow). Humans have the freedom to live in harmony or in conflict, and so need right understanding. This is why the human order is the one that can (and does) disturb nature, and also the one that can fix it.

Note on terminology: I used the course's Hindi terms (padartha, pran, jeev, gyan avastha) from the standard UHV material. The exact one-line "natural characteristics" for each order differ in phrasing between sources, so match them with your class notes.
