Universal Human Values II · Unit IV: Harmony in Nature and Existence

Practice Session and Case Studies

Topic

Terms
Inheritance

Practice: Part B questions for Unit IV and Part A short questions.

Notebook notes
  • Distinguish units of the four orders: in your campus classify stone, soil, water, grass, tree, dog, bird, student, teacher. (e.g., stone, water → material; grass, tree → bio; dog, bird → animal; student, teacher → human — a human also has the body of all lower orders)
  • Case study on recyclability: a village pond with its water cycle, cow-dung manure, crop residue as cattle feed → a closed loop; compare with a city with plastic and garbage dumps (open loop)
  • Case study on self-regulation: forests, rain and rivers, the food chain (predator–prey), loss of vultures / sparrows / bees → cascading imbalance

  • Human being as cause of imbalance — film "Home" (Yann Arthus-Bertrand, 2009) shows how human activity (deforestation, fossil fuels, farming, water use) alters the planet; discuss: pollution, depletion of resources, role of technology (*technology can help (renewable energy) or harm (excess mining), depends on the understanding)

  • Examples to remember (exam favourites): Air, water, soil, rock → material order; Rose, tulsi, trees, grass → bio order; cow, tiger, dog, bird, fish → animal order; human being → human order

  • Inheritance / continuity in each order (what carries the characteristic forward)
    • material order: structure-based (constituents and bonding decide the properties)
    • bio order: seed-based (seed → plant → seed)
    • animal order: breed-based (behaviour fixed as per breed)
    • human order: sanskar-based (values, understanding and education — not fixed by birth)
  • Why the human order is not mutually fulfilling to the other three: wrong understanding (nature seen only as resource), "I am the body" thinking, accumulation as prosperity, technology used without values, ignoring recyclability and self-regulation
Explanation

For "human being as cause of imbalance", structure the answer as: cause (greed, wrong understanding of prosperity) → effects (pollution, global warming, resource depletion) → role of technology (a tool: used wrongly it accelerates; used rightly it helps) → solution (right understanding + recyclable, self-regulating production). The film Home (2009) is mentioned in the syllabus for discussion.
