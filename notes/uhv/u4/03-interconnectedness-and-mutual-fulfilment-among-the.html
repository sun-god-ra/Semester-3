Interconnectedness and Mutual Fulfilment Among the Four Orders

Universal Human Values II · Unit IV: Harmony in Nature and Existence

Interconnectedness and Mutual Fulfilment Among the Four Orders
Subject
Universal Human Values II (24UC201)
Unit
Unit IV: Harmony in Nature and Existence
Topic
3 of 7

Practice: Part B questions for Unit IV and Part A short questions.

Notebook notes
  • Mutual fulfilment = each unit contributes to the others and gets what it needs from them — a natural harmony
    • Material order → Bio order: soil, water, air, sunlight feed the plants
    • Bio order → Animal and human order: plants give food, oxygen, medicine, wood
    • Animal order → Bio order: animals give manure and pollinate (bees, birds), disperse seeds
    • Human order: should protect (nurture) all; uses material, plants, animals in a limited way, and enriches the soil and plants (by right use), gives back (grows plants, cares for animals)
    • Material order ← all: dead plants/animals decay into soil
  • Interconnectedness — the four orders are in harmony: the loss of one (e.g., bees) affects the others (pollination → crops → humans)
  • Human being's role: to ensure mutual fulfilment (understand the harmony and live accordingly) — not to exploit nature; today, the human order is the cause of imbalance (pollution, depletion) because humans act without understanding
Explanation

Draw the arrows: material → bio (nutrients), bio → animal/human (food, oxygen), animal → bio (manure, pollination), all → material (decay). The point: the four orders are mutually dependent and the human should be the one who ensures this fulfilment, as the only one who can understand it. Example: bees pollinate flowers and get nectar — both fulfilled; a human who destroys the hive harms both bees and food supply.
