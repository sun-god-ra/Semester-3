Recyclability and Self-Regulation in Nature

Universal Human Values II · Unit IV: Harmony in Nature and Existence

Recyclability and Self-Regulation in Nature
Subject
Universal Human Values II (24UC201)
Unit
Unit IV: Harmony in Nature and Existence
Topic
4 of 7

Practice: Part B questions for Unit IV and Part A short questions.

Notebook notes
  • Recyclability = materials and energy circulate in nature: what is used returns (waste of one unit is the input of another) → no waste in nature
    • examples: water cycle (evaporation → clouds → rain → rivers → sea), carbon cycle (CO₂ ↔ O₂ between plants and animals/humans), nitrogen cycle, soil → plant → animal → manure → soil, leaf litter → humus, dead bodies → soil
    • Human-made materials (plastic, e-waste, nuclear waste) are not recycled by nature → pollution
  • Self-regulation = each order and each unit maintains a balance (in quantity and relationship) on its own
    • examples: food chain (predator–prey populations are balanced), ecological balance (forests regulate rain, soil, temperature), seed–tree (one seed becomes a tree and gives many seeds only up to a limit), body temperature in animals, atmosphere composition
    • humans disturb this (deforestation, over-fishing) → imbalance
  • Both recyclability and self-regulation → the "harmony" in nature; human should understand and conform*
Recyclability Self-regulation
Meaning matter and energy cycle (waste → input) balance in quantity and relationship is maintained on its own
Examples water, carbon, nitrogen cycles; compost predator–prey balance, forests and rain, population control
Human disturbance plastic, e-waste, nuclear waste deforestation, over-hunting, pollution
Explanation

Recyclability = cycle (waste of one is food of another). Self-regulation = balance (numbers and quantities stay in range). Nature has no garbage: a dead leaf is food for the soil. Our man-made materials (plastic, e-waste) do not fit back in the cycle, so we get pollution. For self-regulation use the predator–prey example: wolves keep the deer population in check; if we remove wolves, deer overgraze the forest.
