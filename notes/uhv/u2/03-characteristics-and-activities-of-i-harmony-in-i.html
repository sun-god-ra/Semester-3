Characteristics and Activities of 'I'; Harmony in 'I'

Universal Human Values II · Unit II: Harmony in the Human Being: Harmony in Myself

Characteristics and Activities of 'I'; Harmony in 'I'
Subject
Universal Human Values II (24UC201)
Unit
Unit II: Harmony in the Human Being: Harmony in Myself
Topic
3 of 7
Terms
Natural acceptance

Practice: Part B questions for Unit II and Part A short questions.

Notebook notes
  • Activities of 'I' (as given in the Gaur–Sangal–Bagaria text; flagged: other books regroup these, so match your lecture notes)
    • Desire — what I want (imagination)
    • Thought — analysing how to get it (imagination)
    • Expectation — selecting / judging what outcome I want from others and from myself (imagination, often pre-conditioned)
    • Natural acceptance — what I accept from within (basic, constant)
    • Realisation and Understanding — knowing the truth of myself and of existence, and living by it (right understanding)
    • Desire, thought and expectation together are called the imagination of 'I'; they are pre-conditioned at present, and should be made consistent with natural acceptance
  • Characteristics of 'I': it is sentient (knows and feels), continuous (exists as long as the human being), has the activities above, and its needs are qualitative (happiness)
  • Natural acceptance includes the values: trust, respect, affection, care, guidance, reverence, glory, gratitude, love (nine, explained in Unit III)
  • Harmony in 'I' = no contradiction between natural acceptance, desire, thought and expectation (all four in line with natural acceptance / right understanding) — leads to continuous happiness
    • If my desire, thought and expectations are in line with my natural acceptance → happiness (harmony)
    • If they contradict each other and my natural acceptance → unhappiness (conflict, stress)
  • Happiness = being in harmony in 'I'; unhappiness = being in conflict in 'I'
  • Natural acceptance (A) vs desire (B) vs thought (C) vs expectation (D) — Check using A–D (see Explain)
Explanation

The simplest working test is natural acceptance vs what I desire, think and expect. Example: natural acceptance says "I should respect my parents", but my desire is to ignore them, my thought is "they don't understand me", my expectation is that they should obey me. These four are not in line, so there is conflict in 'I' = unhappiness. When I work on my thoughts and expectations to match my natural acceptance, the conflict goes away and I feel harmony = happiness. So happiness is not obtained from outside; it depends on the alignment inside 'I'.

A note on terminology: different textbooks list the activities of 'I' a little differently (some list desire, thought, expectation for the three, plus selection and realisation and understanding as higher ones; the Hindi terms chitran, vichar, chayan, anubhuti are also used). In the answer, name the activities you are taught in class and then clearly show the harmony test. The exact grouping of these terms is not something I can verify against your prescribed book page by page, so check it with your lecture notes.
