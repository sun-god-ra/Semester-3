Practice Session — Natural Acceptance vs Liking–Disliking

Universal Human Values II · Unit I: Introduction, Need, and Basic Guidelines

Practice Session — Natural Acceptance vs Liking–Disliking
Subject
Universal Human Values II (24UC201)
Unit
Unit I: Introduction, Need, and Basic Guidelines
Topic
7 of 7
Terms
Natural acceptance

Practice: Part B questions for Unit I and Part A short questions.

Notebook notes
  • Natural acceptance is innate acceptance for living with responsibility (living in relationship, harmony, co-existence) — it is not arbitrary choice based on liking/disliking
  • Exercises: (a) list things you like but know to be wrong (e.g., junk food, late-night phone use) — shows like ≠ natural acceptance; (b) "Do I want to be respected? Do I want to respect others?" → natural acceptance says yes to both; (c) find the difference between what I want to be and what I am
  • Examples to differentiate happiness and prosperity (current vs proposed): a rich man who is always afraid of losing wealth (prosperity in figures, no happiness), a farmer content with his harvest (prosperity in feeling)
  • Case study (basic aspirations): Assumed aspirations — high salary, brand products, popularity. Real aspirations — happiness, respect, trust, harmony; the assumed ones are means, and often misplaced as the goal
Explanation

If a question asks you to "differentiate between what you assume and what you really want", use the same pattern: assumed (job, car, brand) → ask why? → keep asking until you reach happiness, respect, trust → these are the real aspirations and the earlier ones are means. This is the case-study type of question in the syllabus.
