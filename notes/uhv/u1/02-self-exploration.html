Self-Exploration

Universal Human Values II · Unit I: Introduction, Need, and Basic Guidelines

Self-Exploration
Subject
Universal Human Values II (24UC201)
Unit
Unit I: Introduction, Need, and Basic Guidelines
Topic
2 of 7
Terms
Self-exploration, Natural acceptance

Practice: Part B questions for Unit I and Part A short questions.

Notebook notes
  • Self-exploration = process of finding out what is right for me by investigating my own inner reality (my desires, thoughts, expectations) and the outside reality (relationships, nature)
  • Content of self-exploration (what to find out)
    • what do I want to be? — my goal / desire (what I really want in life)
    • what is my program to achieve it? — how I am living today
    • am I able to fulfil it with what I am doing now?
    • i.e. what is "to be done" (i.e. the right thing) and "what I am doing" → find the gap
    • mainly: "What is human being? What does he want? What should he do to get it?"
  • Process of self-exploration (how)
    • uses natural acceptance (inner voice) to check every proposal
    • then experiential validation (live it, see if it gives happiness/harmony in actual living)
    • the two together = mutual verification
  • Natural acceptance = what I naturally accept from within, without any outside pressure, fear, greed or conditioning; it is constant, universal (same for every human), does not change with time, place or situation, and is a feeling in 'I' (not thought)
    • tested by the question "Do I accept this from within?"
    • not the same as liking / disliking (which are preconditioned, changing, based on senses, fashion, company)
  • Experiential validation = live the proposal and see whether it works in living — whether it leads to continuous happiness and harmony. It validates natural acceptance. Done in three levels: (i) in thought (right understanding), (ii) in behaviour (relationships), (iii) in work (production and use of physical facilities)
  • Natural acceptance vs conditioning (pre-conditioning, sanskar) = the beliefs, habits, likes/dislikes that we have acquired from family, media, society. Conditioning is not natural acceptance; it may be right or wrong; to be checked
Natural acceptance Conditioning / liking–disliking
from within, unchanging acquired, changes with time, place, people
same for all human beings different for different persons
leads to harmony, responsibility leads to arbitrariness in choice
"what I want to be" "what I am doing"
the basis for the rest to be evaluated with natural acceptance
  • Examples of natural acceptance: respect, trust, care, being respected and trusted; being right/just; wanting happiness; truth and honesty are accepted (even a liar wants others to be truthful with him); no one wants to be insulted or cheated
Explanation

Natural acceptance vs liking-disliking is the most asked question in unit I. Natural acceptance is from within, constant, the same for all. Liking and disliking come from conditioning (what we have picked up from friends, advertisements, society), and they keep changing. Example: you may like eating fried food, but you accept from within that healthy food is right for your body. Similarly everyone, even a thief, wants to be trusted and respected, and that is natural acceptance.

Experiential validation means that after the inner voice says "this is right", you live it and see if the result is happiness and harmony in your actual life. Example: you accept from within that you should respect your parents; you live it for a month; you check whether your relationship and your own peace of mind improve. If yes, it is validated.

The content of self-exploration is what I want to be (desire), what I am doing (programme), and the gap between them; the process is natural acceptance + experiential validation. A common student mistake is to write that natural acceptance is "what I like"; this is wrong, and the examiner will mark it so.
