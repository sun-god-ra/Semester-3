Trust

Universal Human Values II · Unit III: Harmony in the Family and Society

Trust
Subject
Universal Human Values II (24UC201)
Unit
Unit III: Harmony in the Family and Society
Topic
3 of 8
Terms
Natural acceptance

Practice: Part B questions for Unit III and Part A short questions.

Notebook notes
  • Trust (vishwas) = assuming that the other is intrinsically capable of being right, i.e. his intention is the same as mine at the level of natural acceptance: he wants to be happy and make others happy; whatever is lacking is competence, which can be improved
  • Trust has two parts
    • Intention — what the person wants to do (the natural acceptance; wishes to make me happy / do right); intention is natural — good in everyone
    • Competence — ability to fulfil the intention (skill, understanding, resources); competence is acquired, can be improved
  • Difference between intention and competence
Intention Competence
what the person wants to do (feeling) what the person can do (ability)
is natural, same in all (wants to be right) is acquired, varies from person to person
cannot be seen, assumed/understood can be tested/evaluated
right (good) in everyone needs development (education, practice)
base of Trust base of Respect (evaluating as he is)
  • The mistake today: we judge the other on their competence (what they can do) and conclude about their intention ("he does wrong because he wants to") → mistrust; or we judge our intention vs their competence ("I mean well, he is bad") → conflict
  • Result of trust: both are in harmony and both develop ("you will improve"). Trust is the foundation of relationship and its absence leads to fear, conflict, insecurity
Explanation

Trust = assuming that the other person is basically willing (intention) and able (competence) to do the right thing, in the sense that intention is good in everyone because natural acceptance is the same in all. The classic exam question is "Differentiate intention and competence". The key insight: we tend to judge ourselves on our intention and others on their competence ("I meant well but he made a mistake and is a bad person"). Fixing this one habit improves relations, because intention is good in everyone, and competence can be improved with help and guidance (which is why guidance is a value).
