Understanding Values in Human–Human Relationship

Universal Human Values II · Unit III: Harmony in the Family and Society

Understanding Values in Human–Human Relationship
Subject
Universal Human Values II (24UC201)
Unit
Unit III: Harmony in the Family and Society
Topic
1 of 8
Terms
Natural acceptance

Practice: Part B questions for Unit III and Part A short questions.

Notebook notes
  • Relationship = the feeling in 'I' toward another 'I' (human–human). Every human being lives in a relationship from birth (parents, siblings, friends, teachers, society)
  • Values in relationship = the feelings by which two persons are mutually fulfilled (from natural acceptance); universal values = nine (listed below)
  • Relationship is between 'I' and 'I', not between bodies (not body, wealth, position). Physical facility is exchanged in a relationship, but the relationship itself is the feeling
  • Present problem: we relate on the basis of the Body (age, gender, wealth, status, caste) or on a "give and take" basis → mistrust, conflict
Explanation

A value is a feeling that holds between two 'I's. When we have the right feelings, the relationship fulfils both. The nine are Trust, Respect, Affection, Care, Guidance, Reverence, Glory, Gratitude, Love. Easy way to learn: first two (Trust, Respect) are foundational; next three (Affection, Care, Guidance) are felt toward those who are younger or need help; next three (Reverence, Glory, Gratitude) toward those who are ahead or who have helped; the last (Love) is all of them extended to everyone.

Justice is a four-step idea: recognise the values → live (fulfil) them → evaluate rightly → get mutual happiness. A short exam answer: "Justice is the recognition of values in relationship, their fulfilment, and right evaluation, resulting in mutual happiness." A common mistake is to write that justice means punishment or court judgement. That is not the meaning in this course.

Caution about the definitions: the one-line definitions in the table are given in the style used in UHV classes. Some books phrase them slightly differently (for example the exact wording of affection versus care, or reverence versus glory). I cannot confirm every wording against your prescribed edition, so use the definition given by your faculty for the final answer. The structure (what each is toward, who) is consistent.
