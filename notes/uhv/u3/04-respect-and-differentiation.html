Respect and Differentiation

Universal Human Values II · Unit III: Harmony in the Family and Society

Respect and Differentiation
Subject
Universal Human Values II (24UC201)
Unit
Unit III: Harmony in the Family and Society
Topic
4 of 8
Terms
Natural acceptance

Practice: Part B questions for Unit III and Part A short questions.

Notebook notes
  • Respect (samman) = right evaluation = accepting and valuing the other as he is (his natural acceptance, potential and competence) — neither undervaluing nor overvaluing
    • Over-evaluation (assuming more than he is) → disappointment/ego; under-evaluation (assuming less) → insult; right evaluation → respect
  • Respect ≠ differentiation. Differentiation = treating others differently based on Body, Physical facility, Beliefs (age, gender, caste, colour, wealth, post, religion)
    • Basis of differentiation: body-based (age, gender, height, race, health); physical-facility-based (wealth, post, power, possessions); belief-based (caste, religion, sect, ideology)
    • The result: exploitation, domination, inequality, conflict, wars
Respect Differentiation
Basis 'I' (the Self — potential and competence) Body, physical facility, beliefs (external)
Nature right evaluation (as he is) bias — over- or under-evaluation
Result mutual fulfilment, equality in value exploitation, discrimination, conflict
  • Respect and trust are the foundation of relationship (without them no other value can be lived). In a family, the first thing to check is "Do I trust and respect each member as he is?"
  • Other salient values: affection, care, guidance, reverence, glory, gratitude, love (as in the table above)
    • Gratitude — a universal value to acknowledge contributions of those who helped me (teachers, parents, farmers, soldiers, nature)
    • Love — fulfilment of all: trust and respect for all human beings, "vasudhaiva kutumbakam" (the world is one family)
Explanation

Respect = right evaluation (the other as he is). Differentiation = treating differently on the basis of body, possessions or belief. For example, giving a better seat to a rich guest than to a poor one, or looking down on someone because of caste or accent. Differentiation, not difference in competence, causes the exploitation and conflict that we see. Do not confuse: respect does not mean agreeing with everything or being very polite; it means evaluating correctly.
