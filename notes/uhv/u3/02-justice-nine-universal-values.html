Justice — Nine Universal Values

Universal Human Values II · Unit III: Harmony in the Family and Society

Justice — Nine Universal Values
Subject
Universal Human Values II (24UC201)
Unit
Unit III: Harmony in the Family and Society
Topic
2 of 8

Practice: Part B questions for Unit III and Part A short questions.

Notebook notes
  • Justice = recognition of values in relationship → their fulfilment → right evaluation → mutual happiness. In four steps (the book's four components of Justice):
    1. Recognition of values — knowing the values (the nine) in the relationship (who is the other, what do I feel and expect)
    2. Fulfilment — feeling the values (living them; my feelings for the other are in line with the value)
    3. Evaluation — right evaluation of the other (as he is, not as I assume) and of myself
    4. Mutual happiness — the result: both are happy (since both are fulfilled)
  • So Justice = recognition + fulfilment + evaluation → mutual happiness ("nyay"). Without justice → mutual unhappiness
  • Nine universal values (feelings) in human–human relationship
# Value Meaning (in short, notebook style)
1 Trust (vishwas) assuming the other is intrinsically (basically) willing and able to be right (intention good, can improve)
2 Respect (samman) right evaluation — accepting the other as he is (his competence & potential), neither more nor less
3 Affection (sneh) feeling of being related to the other as one's own (typically toward the younger / junior), wanting the other to grow and be happy
4 Care (mamta) feeling of responsibility for the nurturing, protection of the body of the other (physical facility)
5 Guidance (vatsalya) feeling of responsibility for making the other understand the right (right understanding, behaviour, work)
6 Reverence (shraddha) feeling for the one who is ahead of me in understanding (excellence) and wishes to move to that level
7 Glory (gaurav) feeling for the one who is working for the right understanding of all (he makes others reach the excellence)
8 Gratitude (kritagyata) feeling of acknowledging the contribution of those who helped me (teachers, parents, nature) to understand and live well
9 Love (prem) feeling of being related to all human beings ("all are one"); the extension of the other values to everyone
  • Notes: Affection, care, guidance are towards the junior/equal; Reverence, glory, gratitude are for the senior or helper; Trust and respect are towards all; Love = all — the extension of all others . The wording of each definition varies between teachers — verify against your lecture notes (flagged because exact definitions are taught orally in this course)
  • Justice is the basis for "Order" in family and society (it ensures mutual fulfilment in all relationships)
  • Programme for fulfilment of justice (in relationship) — (i) understand the nine values, (ii) live them in every relationship (family, friends, institution), (iii) evaluate my feelings and behaviour, (iv) ensure the other's happiness → mutual happiness
Explanation

A value is a feeling that holds between two 'I's. When we have the right feelings, the relationship fulfils both. The nine are Trust, Respect, Affection, Care, Guidance, Reverence, Glory, Gratitude, Love. Easy way to learn: first two (Trust, Respect) are foundational; next three (Affection, Care, Guidance) are felt toward those who are younger or need help; next three (Reverence, Glory, Gratitude) toward those who are ahead or who have helped; the last (Love) is all of them extended to everyone.

Justice is a four-step idea: recognise the values → live (fulfil) them → evaluate rightly → get mutual happiness. A short exam answer: "Justice is the recognition of values in relationship, their fulfilment, and right evaluation, resulting in mutual happiness." A common mistake is to write that justice means punishment or court judgement. That is not the meaning in this course.

Caution about the definitions: the one-line definitions in the table are given in the style used in UHV classes. Some books phrase them slightly differently (for example the exact wording of affection versus care, or reverence versus glory). I cannot confirm every wording against your prescribed edition, so use the definition given by your faculty for the final answer. The structure (what each is toward, who) is consistent.
