Comprehensive Human Goals

Universal Human Values II · Unit III: Harmony in the Family and Society

Comprehensive Human Goals
Subject
Universal Human Values II (24UC201)
Unit
Unit III: Harmony in the Family and Society
Topic
6 of 8

Practice: Part B questions for Unit III and Part A short questions.

Notebook notes
  • Four comprehensive human goals (to be achieved at all levels — individual, family, society, nature)
    1. Resolution (samadhan) → right understanding: clarity and the ability to resolve problems, leading to continuous happiness
    2. Prosperity (samriddhi) → feeling of having more than required physical facility (in the family, everyone)
    3. Fearlessness (abhay) → trust in relationships (no fear in society; right relationships)
    4. Co-existence (sah-astitva) → mutual fulfilment between all units (family, society, nature)
  • In one line: "Resolution, Prosperity, Fearlessness (Trust) and Co-existence" are the four goals of a Universal Human Order
  • Mapping to the basic aspirations: Resolution (right understanding) + Prosperity → continuous happiness and prosperity; fearlessness (trust) and co-existence → harmony in relationship and society
Goal Level it appears in Meaning
Resolution individual → whole society right understanding; peaceful resolution of problems
Prosperity individual → family → society feeling of enough (everyone has more than required)
Fearlessness (trust) relationship → society no fear (trust in each other)
Co-existence society → nature mutual fulfilment, interconnectedness
Explanation

Society is just the family extended; if trust and respect work at the family level they work at larger levels. The four comprehensive goals — Resolution (right understanding), Prosperity, Fearlessness (trust), Co-existence — are the aim of an ideal society. The five dimensions are the mechanisms. A neat way to remember the 5 dimensions: Education, Health, Justice, Production, Exchange (each paired with right living, self-regulation, preservation, work, storage).

Undivided society means no walls on the basis of caste, religion, nation. Universal Human Order is this undivided society at every level, from the family to the world family. The syllabus line "Universal Order – from family to world family" is the chain of extension: family → extended family → village/neighbourhood → region → nation → world. The number of steps in the chain differs between sources (some give a ten-level ladder); I could not check the exact count in your prescribed book, so give the idea (extension of the family order), and the count only if your teacher does.
