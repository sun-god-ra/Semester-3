Definitiveness of Ethical Human Conduct

Universal Human Values II · Unit V: Implications on Professional Ethics

Definitiveness of Ethical Human Conduct
Subject
Universal Human Values II (24UC201)
Unit
Unit V: Implications on Professional Ethics
Topic
2 of 6
Terms
Natural acceptance

Practice: Part B questions for Unit V and Part A short questions.

Notebook notes
  • Definitiveness = the conduct of a human being that is ethical has a definite (fixed, universal) basis: values, policy and character
    • Values — the nine universal values (natural acceptance)
    • Policy — the principles / rules (norms) that follow from the values (what to do, how), in education, health, justice, production, exchange
    • Character — living by the values and policy in thought, behaviour, work and understanding (consistently, in all situations)
  • Ethical human conduct = definite (the same for all, not relative to place, time or person) — it is based on universal values, not on personal liking
  • Present (conventional) view of ethics: relative, depends on society, rules, religion, convenience → "what is accepted by society"; UHV view: based on natural acceptance and right understanding → definite
  • Humanistic = based on human values and the right understanding of the human being. The next three items are its implementation in society
Aspect Meaning
Values the universal feelings (nine)
Policy rules and decisions that follow from values
Character actual living according to values and policy
Explanation

Ethics here is not a rulebook handed down from outside. It is living according to what we already accept from within (natural acceptance of human values). That is why it is called definitive: it is the same for all, unlike customs that differ from place to place. Values → policy → character is the order: values are the what I feel, policy is the what I decide to do, and character is what I actually live. A one-line exam answer: ethical human conduct is the definite conduct of a human being based on universal human values, expressed through values, policy and character.
