What is an Agile Process?

Software Engineering · Unit I: Process Models and Agile Development

What is an Agile Process?
Subject
Software Engineering (24CS203)
Unit
Unit I: Process Models and Agile Development
Topic
10 of 13
Terms
Agile, Refactoring, Continuous integration

Practice: Part B questions for Unit I and Part A short questions.

Notebook notes
  • Addresses unpredictability: requirements & priorities change; analysis, design, construction not predictable → adaptability
  • Characteristics: incremental development in short iterations, customer feedback on each increment, small teams, minimal documentation (just enough), face-to-face communication
  • Agility principles (Pressman): customer satisfaction through early delivery, welcome change, frequent delivery, collaboration, motivated individuals, trust, face-to-face, working software as the measure, sustainable development, technical excellence, simplicity, self-organizing teams, reflection
  • Human factors: competence, common focus, collaboration, decision-making ability, fuzzy problem-solving ability, mutual trust and respect, self-organisation
Explanation

Agility means responding effectively to change. The Agile Manifesto states four preferences: individuals and interactions over processes and tools, working software over comprehensive documentation, customer collaboration over contract negotiation, and responding to change over following a plan. The items on the right still have value, but those on the left are valued more.

Agile methods challenge the cost-of-change curve. In conventional development, a change costs far more when made late, because it affects documents, designs and code that were built on earlier decisions. Agile proponents say that with short iterations, constant testing, refactoring and continuous integration, the curve flattens and a late change costs only slightly more than an early one. This claim is debated, particularly for large architectural changes. An agile process is incremental, so a working increment is delivered in short cycles, and customer feedback on each increment guides the next one.
