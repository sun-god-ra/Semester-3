Other Agile Process Models

Software Engineering · Unit I: Process Models and Agile Development

Other Agile Process Models
Subject
Software Engineering (24CS203)
Unit
Unit I: Process Models and Agile Development
Topic
12 of 13
Terms
Scrum, Agile, Unified process

Practice: Part B questions for Unit I and Part A short questions.

Notebook notes
Model Key idea
Adaptive Software Development (ASD) (Jim Highsmith) self-organizing, collaborative teams; cycle: speculation, collaboration, learning; mission-driven, component-based, time-boxed
Scrum (Schwaber, Sutherland) sprints (2–4 weeks) + backlog. Roles: Product Owner, Scrum Master, Development Team. Events: sprint planning, daily scrum (15 min), sprint review, sprint retrospective. Artifacts: product backlog, sprint backlog, increment
Dynamic Systems Development Method (DSDM) 80/20 (Pareto) rule: 80 % of application in 20 % of the time; time-boxing, MoSCoW prioritisation (Must, Should, Could, Won't); phases: feasibility study, business study, functional model iteration, design & build iteration, implementation
Crystal (Alistair Cockburn) family of methods by team size and criticality (Clear, Yellow, Orange, Red); people-centric; "process = people"
Feature Driven Development (FDD) (Peter Coad) feature = client-valued function (action-result-object), 2 weeks max; five steps: develop overall model, build feature list, plan by feature, design by feature, build by feature
Lean Software Development (LSD) from lean manufacturing: eliminate waste, build quality in, create knowledge, defer commitment, deliver fast, respect people, optimise the whole
Agile Modeling (AM) "just enough" modeling; practices: model with a purpose, use multiple models, travel light, content more important than representation
Agile Unified Process (AUP) serial in the large, iterative in the small; simplified UP
Explanation

Scrum organises work in sprints of two to four weeks. The Product Owner maintains the product backlog, the team selects items for the sprint backlog, and a short daily scrum of about 15 minutes keeps everyone synchronised. At the end of the sprint there is a review of the increment and a retrospective on how the team worked. DSDM uses time-boxing and the 80/20 rule, saying 80% of the system can be delivered in 20% of the time, and prioritises requirements as Must, Should, Could and Won't have. Crystal is a family of methods that are chosen according to team size and project criticality. Feature Driven Development builds the system feature by feature in five steps, where a feature is a small client-valued function. Lean development takes the principles of lean manufacturing, in particular the elimination of waste. Adaptive Software Development works in cycles of speculation, collaboration and learning.
