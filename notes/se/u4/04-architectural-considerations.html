Architectural Considerations

Software Engineering · Unit IV: Software Design

Architectural Considerations
Subject
Software Engineering (24CS203)
Unit
Unit IV: Software Design
Topic
4 of 12
Terms
Abstraction, Data flow diagram

Practice: Part B questions for Unit IV and Part A short questions.

Notebook notes
  • Economy — keep the architecture simple: minimal complexity; Visibility — decisions and relationships explicit (visible to stakeholders); Spacing — separation of concerns, logical separation; Symmetry — the architecture's consistent use of structures; Emergence — complex behaviour emerges from simple parts
  • Quality attributes influencing architecture: performance, security, availability, usability, modifiability, testability, interoperability, scalability, portability, reliability
  • Architectural tactics to achieve attributes (e.g. redundancy for availability, caching for performance)
  • Trade-off analysis: attributes conflict (security vs performance) → ATAM
Explanation

Quality attributes such as performance, security, availability and modifiability influence the choice of architecture, and they often conflict, so trade-offs must be made. For this the Architecture Trade-off Analysis Method (ATAM) evaluates candidate architectures against scenarios and finds where attributes are sensitive to architectural choices.

The first step of architectural design is to place the system in context. The architectural context diagram shows the system with the external systems it interacts with: superordinate systems that use it, subordinate systems that it uses, peers that exchange information with it, and the actors. Next, archetypes are defined, abstractions of the core elements, such as a sensor, an indicator, a controller and a node. The archetypes are refined into components, and the architecture is instantiated for a specific problem to demonstrate it.

An architecture can be derived from a data flow diagram. In transform flow, data enters, is converted at a transform centre, and leaves. In transaction flow, one incoming item triggers one of several possible action paths, and a transaction centre chooses which. In each case the DFD is factored into a hierarchy of controllers and workers.
