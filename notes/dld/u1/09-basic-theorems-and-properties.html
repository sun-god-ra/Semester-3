Notebook notes
Law Form 1 (OR) Form 2 (AND)
Identity \(x+0=x\) \(x\cdot1=x\)
Null / dominance \(x+1=1\) \(x\cdot0=0\)
Idempotent \(x+x=x\) \(x\cdot x=x\)
Complement \(x+x'=1\) \(x\cdot x'=0\)
Involution \((x')'=x\)
Commutative \(x+y=y+x\) \(xy=yx\)
Associative \(x+(y+z)=(x+y)+z\) \(x(yz)=(xy)z\)
Distributive \(x(y+z)=xy+xz\) \(x+yz=(x+y)(x+z)\)
De Morgan \((x+y)'=x'y'\) \((xy)'=x'+y'\)
Absorption \(x+xy=x\) \(x(x+y)=x\)
Simplification \(x+x'y=x+y\) \(x(x'+y)=xy\)
Consensus \(xy+x'z+yz=xy+x'z\) \((x+y)(x'+z)(y+z)=(x+y)(x'+z)\)
  • De Morgan for \(n\) variables: \((ABC\ldots)'=A'+B'+C'+\ldots\) and \((A+B+C+\ldots)'=A'B'C'\ldots\)
  • Proof by truth table (perfect induction) or by algebra
  • Example: \(A+A'B=(A+A')(A+B)=A+B\)
Explanation

Boolean algebra was designed for logic, with two values and three operations. Two features make it different from ordinary algebra. First, \(x+x=x\) and \(x\cdot x=x\), since repeating a statement adds nothing. Second, OR distributes over AND (\(x+yz=(x+y)(x+z)\)), which has no counterpart in ordinary algebra.

The principle of duality halves the learning. Each theorem has a twin obtained by swapping AND with OR and 0 with 1. De Morgan's laws are the most used theorems: the complement of an OR is the AND of complements, and the other way round. They let us convert between AND-OR forms and NAND/NOR forms. Absorption (\(x+xy=x\)) and the simplification \(x+x'y=x+y\) are the two identities that do most of the work when simplifying expressions by hand.
