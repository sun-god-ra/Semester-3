Product-of-Sums Simplification

Digital Logic Design · Unit II: Boolean Function Simplification and Combinational Logic Design

Product-of-Sums Simplification
Subject
Digital Logic Design (24CS201)
Unit
Unit II: Boolean Function Simplification and Combinational Logic Design
Topic
3 of 10
Terms
De morgan, Sum of products

Practice: Part B questions for Unit II and Part A short questions.

Notebook notes
  • To get POS: group the 0s → get \(F'\) in SOP → complement with De Morgan
  • Or directly: from a group of 0s, write a sum term where variable that is 0 in the group appears uncomplemented and variable that is 1 appears complemented
  • Example: same \(F=\sum m(0,1,2,5,8,9,10)\). Zeros: 3,4,6,7,11,12,13,14,15 → \(F'=AB+CD+BD'\) →

\[F=(A'+B')(C'+D')(B'+D)\]

  • Use POS when it needs fewer gates, or when the output is needed as OR-AND (NOR-NOR) form
  • \(F=\sum m(\ldots)=\prod M(\text{the rest})\)
Explanation

Grouping the 1s gives a sum of products. To get a product of sums, group the 0s instead. That simplifies the complement \(F'\), and by De Morgan's law \(F=(F')'\) turns the sum of products of \(F'\) into a product of sums. When you read a sum term directly from a group of 0s, the sign is reversed compared with SOP: a variable that is 0 throughout the group is written uncomplemented, since the sum must be 0 there. Choose POS or SOP according to whichever has fewer groups, or whichever matches the gates you have.
