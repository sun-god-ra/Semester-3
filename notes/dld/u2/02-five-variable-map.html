Five-Variable Map

Digital Logic Design · Unit II: Boolean Function Simplification and Combinational Logic Design

Five-Variable Map
Subject
Digital Logic Design (24CS201)
Unit
Unit II: Boolean Function Simplification and Combinational Logic Design
Topic
2 of 10
Terms
Minterm

Practice: Part B questions for Unit II and Part A short questions.

Notebook notes
  • Two 4-variable maps side by side: \(A=0\) (minterms 0–15) and \(A=1\) (minterms 16–31)
  • Cells in the same position in both maps are adjacent (like looking through one map onto the other). Also the usual wrap-around inside each map
  • Mirror-image adjacency: cell \(i\) (in left) and cell \(i+16\) (in right)
  • Group sizes up to 32
  • Example: \(F=\sum m(0,2,4,6,9,11,13,15,17,21,25,27,29,31)\) gives \(F=BE+AD'E+A'B'E'\)
Explanation

Five variables give 32 cells, which we draw as two 4-variable maps, one for \(A=0\) and one for \(A=1\). Besides the usual neighbours inside each map, two cells in the same position on the two maps are neighbours as well, so a group may extend from one map to the other. It is helpful to imagine the right map lying on top of the left map. The method is the same as for four variables, only with more places to look for groups, and this is why larger problems are handed to the tabulation method.
