Binary Parallel Adder

Digital Logic Design · Unit III: Combinational Circuits and Programmable Logic Devices

Binary Parallel Adder
Subject
Digital Logic Design (24CS201)
Unit
Unit III: Combinational Circuits and Programmable Logic Devices
Topic
1 of 9
Terms
Full adder, Carry look-ahead, XOR

Practice: Part B questions for Unit III and Part A short questions.

Notebook notes
  • Adds two \(n\)-bit numbers at once using \(n\) full adders in a chain. Carry out of stage \(i\) → carry in of stage \(i+1\)
  • Ripple-carry adder: simplest, but slow — each stage waits for the carry of the previous one. Delay \(\approx n\times\) (carry delay of one FA)
  • 4-bit adder: 7483 IC. Inputs \(A_3..A_0\), \(B_3..B_0\), \(C_0\); outputs \(S_3..S_0\), \(C_4\)
  • Carry look-ahead adder (fast)
    • Carry generate \(G_i=A_iB_i\) — stage makes a carry by itself
    • Carry propagate \(P_i=A_i\oplus B_i\) — stage passes the carry on
    • \(S_i=P_i\oplus C_i\) and \(C_{i+1}=G_i+P_iC_i\)
    • Expand so that no carry waits for another:

\[\begin{aligned}C_1&=G_0+P_0C_0\\C_2&=G_1+P_1G_0+P_1P_0C_0\\C_3&=G_2+P_2G_1+P_2P_1G_0+P_2P_1P_0C_0\\C_4&=G_3+P_3G_2+P_3P_2G_1+P_3P_2P_1G_0+P_3P_2P_1P_0C_0\end{aligned}\]

  • All carries are ready after 2–3 gate delays, independent of \(n\). Cost: more gates, large fan-in
  • Binary adder–subtractor: XOR each \(B\) bit with a mode input \(M\), and use \(M\) as carry-in
    • \(M=0\): \(A+B\). \(M=1\): \(B\oplus1=B'\), \(C_0=1\) → \(A+B'+1=A-B\) (2's complement)
    • Overflow \(V=C_{n}\oplus C_{n-1}\) (carry into and out of sign bit) — for signed numbers
Explanation

A full adder handles one column of an addition. To add two \(n\)-bit numbers, we chain \(n\) full adders, connecting each carry out to the next carry in. The inputs are applied in parallel and the sum bits come out together, which is why it is called a parallel adder. The weakness is the carry chain. The top stage cannot produce a correct sum until the carry has travelled through every earlier stage, so the delay grows with the width of the numbers. For 64 bits the delay would be unacceptable.

Carry look-ahead removes that wait by computing every carry directly from the inputs. For each stage we ask two questions. Does this stage create a carry on its own? That happens when both bits are 1, which is the generate term \(G_i=A_iB_i\). Will it pass on a carry that arrives? That happens when exactly one bit is 1, which is the propagate term \(P_i=A_i\oplus B_i\). A carry leaves stage \(i\) if the stage generates one, or if it propagates one that arrived. Substituting repeatedly gives each carry as a two-level expression in the \(G\)'s, \(P\)'s and \(C_0\), so all carries appear at roughly the same time.

An adder can also subtract. XOR each bit of \(B\) with a control \(M\) and feed \(M\) into the first carry in. When \(M=0\) the bits pass through unchanged and the circuit adds. When \(M=1\) the bits are inverted and a 1 is added at the bottom, which is the 2's complement of \(B\), so the circuit computes \(A-B\). For signed numbers, overflow is flagged when the carry into the sign bit differs from the carry out.
