Demultiplexers

Digital Logic Design · Unit III: Combinational Circuits and Programmable Logic Devices

Demultiplexers
Subject
Digital Logic Design (24CS201)
Unit
Unit III: Combinational Circuits and Programmable Logic Devices
Topic
5 of 9
Terms
Decoder, Demultiplexer

Practice: Part B questions for Unit III and Part A short questions.

Notebook notes
  • Demux: 1 data input → \(2^n\) outputs, \(n\) select lines choose which output gets the data (opposite of MUX)
  • 1-to-4 demux: \(Y_0=D\,S_1'S_0'\), \(Y_1=D\,S_1'S_0\), \(Y_2=D\,S_1S_0'\), \(Y_3=D\,S_1S_0\)
  • Same circuit as a decoder: use the data as the enable
  • Uses: serial → parallel conversion, routing
Explanation

A demultiplexer sends one input to one of several outputs, chosen by the select lines. It is the same circuit as a decoder when the data input takes the role of the enable: the decoder activates one output line, and when the data is 1 the line is on, and when the data is 0 it is off. This is why decoder ICs are often labelled "decoder/demultiplexer".
