Timing Sequences

Digital Logic Design · Unit V: Registers, Counters, and Memory Units

Timing Sequences
Subject
Digital Logic Design (24CS201)
Unit
Unit V: Registers, Counters, and Memory Units
Topic
7 of 10
Terms
Ring counter, Decoder, Johnson counter, Flip-flop

Practice: Part B questions for Unit V and Part A short questions.

Notebook notes
  • Control unit of a computer needs timing signals \(T_0,T_1,T_2,\ldots\) — one active per clock period, in order, to sequence operations (fetch, decode, execute)
  • Generated by: (1) ring counter; (2) \(n\)-bit counter + \(n\)-to-\(2^n\) decoder (fewer FFs, but decoder glitches); (3) Johnson counter + AND gates
  • Counter + decoder: 2-bit counter and 2-to-4 decoder → four timing signals \(T_0..T_3\)
  • A sequence can be reset (clear) by control and repeats. Timing signals + control inputs (AND) → control functions
  • Timing diagram shows the waveforms: clock, count, each \(T_i\)
Explanation

The control unit of a digital computer must perform operations in a fixed order, and for that it needs signals \(T_0,T_1,T_2,\ldots\) where only one is active at a time. There are two ways to generate them: a ring counter, which gives them directly but uses many flip-flops, or a counter followed by a decoder, which uses fewer flip-flops but may produce glitches in the decoder. The timing signals are ANDed with the conditions of the instruction to produce control signals that act at the right clock pulse.
